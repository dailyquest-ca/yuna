# Yuna — conformance and quality audit

*Against `docs/yuna_plan.md` at the **2026-07-31 16:37 UTC** stamp. Repo at `51f75b9`.*

Method: fourteen independent auditors, eight owning a slice of the plan line by line and six
judging build quality by discipline, then an adversarial pass on every critical and major claim.
The findings below are the ones I re-verified myself against the code — this codebase's history
is that plausible-sounding claims survive until someone checks them, so nothing here is relayed
on trust.

Raw: **262 findings** — 27 critical, 161 major, 74 minor. Deduplicated and ranked by
consequence, they collapse into the twelve problems in Part 2.

---

## Part 1 — Does the build match the plan?

**No.** Not yet, and the gap is wider than the ledger claimed before this audit.

| | |
|---|---|
| Plan clauses catalogued | 89 |
| Implemented in code | 53 |
| **Actually reached by a running job** | **31** |
| Not implemented | 34 |
| Awaiting a ruling from Zak | 0 |

The middle row is the finding. Twenty-two rules exist, are tested, and **nothing calls them**.

### The shape of the problem

The build has two of almost everything. `policy.py` holds the plan's rules as pure, tested
functions. The job modules hold their own inline copies, written earlier. Seventeen of the
thirty policy functions have no caller anywhere, and where both exist the job's copy is the one
that runs — the untested one, drifted from the plan.

This is my error and it is the root cause of most of Part 2. The refactor extracted the rules
correctly and then stopped short of replacing the originals, which produced the worst of both
arrangements: a test suite that proves the *right* rules work, over a system that runs the
*wrong* ones.

The ledger was supposed to catch exactly this and did not, because `wired` was a boolean I typed
by hand. It was wrong in nine places. It is now derived from a static call graph and a
conformance test fails when the declared flag disagrees, so it cannot drift again. That fix is
already committed; the corrected count is the 31 above.

## Part 2 — What must be fixed

Ranked by what it costs if left alone. Every item was verified against the code, not inferred.

### 1. Gate C1 excludes the compounders the plan names as eligible — CRITICAL

§3.1: *"Banks and insurers excluded — vendor industries named `Banks - …` or `Insurance - …`;
**Insurance Brokers, Credit Services, Capital Markets and the rest of Financial Services remain
eligible**."*

`fundamentals.py:30` excludes the **entire Financial Services sector**, plus a word list that
names `"capital markets"` and `"credit services"` explicitly:

```python
FIN_SECTORS = {"financial services", "financials", "financial"}
FIN_WORDS = ("bank", "insur", "capital markets", "credit services", "reinsur", ...)
is_fin = (sector or "").lower() in FIN_SECTORS or ... or any(w in hay for w in FIN_WORDS)
```

This removes Visa, Mastercard, S&P Global, Moody's, MSCI, Schwab and every exchange from the
compounder universe — the toll-booth businesses the sleeve is built to own. `policy.is_excluded_financial`
implements the plan correctly, has tests, and is never called.

**Fix:** call the policy function. One line, plus a re-sweep.

### 2. The momentum entry ticket buys the whole position — CRITICAL

§3.2's pyramid: step 1 at the pivot break is **50%**, steps 2 and 3 add 25% each.

`phase0.py:163-168` computes `size_pct` — the *full* intended position under §3.2 sizing — and
writes that entire quantity into the entry ticket. A stop firing on the day of entry costs the
full risk budget instead of half, and the position never has a step 2 or 3 to arm.

**Fix:** the entry ticket carries 50% of `size_pct`; `policy.pyramid_orders` already produces
steps 2 and 3.

### 3. The live base detector is the superseded rule — CRITICAL

`rank.py:64-78` is the source of every momentum pivot, entry price and initial stop. It diverges
from §3.2 three ways:

| | Plan v4 | `rank.py` |
|---|---|---|
| Pivot window | highest high **120 → 25** sessions back | trailing **120**, last 25 included |
| Break test | **any** later close above the pivot | only the **last** close |
| 0.5% grace | applies to **highs** | applied to a **close** |

The window bug costs money: a spike three days ago steals the pivot from the real base beneath
it, `blen>=25` then marks the name invalid, and a genuine setup silently disappears. This is a
plausible mechanism for the backtest's central finding that the machine would not deploy capital.

`policy.scan_base` implements the amended rule, has ten tests, and is not called.

### 4. M1 — the sleeve master switch — is never enforced at entry — CRITICAL

§3.2 gate table: *"M1 — Market stage · Sleeve master switch · Weekly close; **enforced at entry
time**."* §6 Step 4: *"As real setups fire, **gate permitting**."*

`gate_state` appears in `phase0.py` **zero** times. Momentum entry tickets are written with no
gate check, so the sleeve can be armed into a bear tape — the one thing the Weinstein gate exists
to prevent.

### 5. Phase 0 buys names Zak never approved — CRITICAL

§6 Step 3: *"**Approved** bench names at/below hurdle enter immediately."* §3.1's funnel requires
a C2 memo and Zak's sign-off before a name joins the bench.

`phase0.py` reads the `bench` table without ever selecting `approved` — the word appears zero
times in the file. Every at-or-below-hurdle candidate becomes a buy ticket, C2 unwritten.

### 6. The sleeve assignment is computed and thrown away — CRITICAL

§6 Step 2a assigns every holding to a sleeve. `phase0.py:101` computes `sleeve_assigned` and the
write block that follows inserts only into `briefs` and `tickets` — there is no `update book`
anywhere in the file.

`daily.py:56` selects `where sleeve='momentum'` to run the stop ratchet. With `book.sleeve` never
written, **no position is ever protected by a trailing stop.** The entire §3.2 stop machinery is
live, correct, and operating on an empty set.

### 7. No corporate-action refresh — a split reads as a 75% crash — CRITICAL

§4.1 states it twice, once in the feed table and once in the imperative: *"a split rewrites a
stock's entire adjusted history — any split/dividend event triggers a one-call re-pull of that
name. Without it, a 4:1 split reads as a −75% crash and fires false alarms."*

No splits or dividends endpoint is called anywhere. `ingest.py` fetches strictly forward from the
newest bar it holds, so historical adjusted closes are never rewritten. The failure mode is the
one the plan spells out, and it fires a sell.

### 8. No price validation of any kind — CRITICAL

§4.1's quarantine rule — *"any print moving > 40% with no corporate action, or any print that
would fire a sell-side action, needs two sources to agree"* — is implemented for balances (in the
new write path) and for prices **not at all**. `ingest.py:87` writes vendor OHLC verbatim with no
bounds check and no comparison to the prior close.

One bad print sets `highest_close`, which ratchets the trail up permanently, and the stop never
comes back down.

### 9. A killed job reports green — CRITICAL

`db.Heartbeat` opens a row as `'running'` and closes it green/amber/red. `daily.freshness` treats
only `'red'` and `'amber'` as stale. A runner killed by timeout or cancellation leaves `'running'`
forever, which matches neither list, and the next brief opens *"✓ all green"* over a half-written
price table — with §5.6's *"stale data ⇒ no new tickets"* consequently not firing.

Two related holes: the `if: failure()` autopsy cannot run when the failure precedes
`pip install -e .`, and **nothing detects a job that never ran at all** — a job that does not fire
writes no row, so it cannot appear in any staleness check.

### 10. The nightly ingest is exactly what §4.1 forbids — MAJOR

§4.1: *"Prices in bulk — a few hundred calls nightly regardless of universe size, **never 1,500
per-ticker pulls as the routine.** Per-ticker calls remain the tool for exactly four things."*

`ingest.py:15` uses only the single-ticker endpoint and loops the whole active universe. This is
the routine, at roughly 2,800 calls a night against a 100,000/day quota — survivable, but it is
the named anti-pattern, and there is no quota meter and no 70% alarm either.

### 11. The database will not stop a wrong number — MAJOR

- **Zero CHECK constraints** across the schema. Every state machine — ticket states, sleeve names,
  run statuses — is a SQL comment. The new write path validates inside its verb bodies, so any
  other writer bypasses it entirely.
- **139 float columns, zero numeric**, in the tables that hold actual dollars.
- **Views run with definer rights.** No view sets `security_invoker`, and no RLS policy exists
  anywhere. RLS is enabled on 24 tables with no policies, which is correctly default-deny for the
  *tables* — but a view owned by the migrator reads straight through it. §4.8's *"RLS default-deny
  — nothing publicly reachable"* is true of tables and not of `v_book`.
- **`v_fundamentals_latest` is still `select *`** — the exact construct that already broke this
  system once, with only a comment preventing a recurrence.

### 12. The backtest tests rules the plan deleted — MAJOR

The only integration-level evidence about this strategy simulates pyramid bands of +2.5%/+4.5%
(the plan says +2%/+4%, both limited at pivot × 1.05), fills adds above the ceiling, and dumps
unconfirmed breakouts the next morning. It also ranks on a bar's own close and then trades that
same bar — lookahead on every re-rank day.

Its conclusions are not currently evidence about the system as specified.

### Also confirmed, lower consequence

- `score.py` runs **weekly** inside `monthly-funnel`, so §3.1's *"two consecutive months outside
  the top 60"* eviction seatbelt fires in two weeks.
- The **compounder pipeline screens a looser universe than the momentum pipeline** — `funnel.py`
  sets `in_l0` at $4 and one day's $5M volume with no listing-age test, and `score.py` never
  re-applies §3.0's real filters.
- The **earnings blackout is implemented four times**, none of them 5 trading days; the version
  that writes real tickets hardcodes 8 calendar days.
- **`transactions` has never been written to** — §4.3's ledger of every confirmed fill.
- **The 3-year bar window is not pruned or archived**; `prices` grows without bound.
- **`weekly-rank` can die on a duplicate `queue` primary key** whenever a holding is also a top-10
  BUY candidate, leaving last week's trigger prices live.
- **`nightly-retry` re-runs only the bar pull**, never the `daily` duties — so the night the
  pipeline fails is the night with no stop sheet.
- **UTC is stated but pinned nowhere**; 14 uses of `current_date`/`::date` resolve in whatever
  timezone the session carries, and the jobs run at 02:00 UTC.
- **A missing USD/CAD rate silently becomes 1.0**, understating NAV by roughly a quarter.
- **`fundamentals.yml` carries a sixth cron** where §4.2 authorizes five.

## Part 3 — Quality verdict

Zak's mandate: *"YOU are completely in charge of architecture and build quality."* So, plainly.

### What is genuinely good

- **The heartbeat contract.** One `runs` row per job, tracebacks embedded, an autopsy step for
  deaths before it opens. Given that Actions log downloads 403 on this repo, this is the only
  diagnostic that survives, and it was the right thing to build first.
- **Point-in-time fundamentals.** Every statement stored with its own `filing_date`. That is a
  genuine asset most retail systems never build, and it is what makes the compounder side
  honestly backtestable as the system runs.
- **The session write path (migration 018).** The best-engineered artifact in the repo. A role
  with zero table privileges, `search_path` pinned with `pg_temp` last on all 21 definer
  functions, config protection layered three ways, quarantine thresholds as constants rather than
  config so a session cannot widen its own guard — and it names its own residue instead of hiding
  it.
- **The rule/plumbing split is the right architecture.** The problem is that it is half-applied,
  not that it is wrong.
- **Comment quality.** The code explains *why*, cites plan sections, and records the defect that
  motivated each guard. That is rare and it is why this audit could be precise.

### What is not good enough

**Architecture — the dual-implementation problem is the whole story.** Seventeen rules exist
twice; the tested copy is dead and the drifted copy runs. Everything in Part 2 items 1, 2, 3 and
the blackout duplication is a symptom. The fix is not new code — it is deleting the inline copies
and calling `policy`. That is a day of careful work and it closes roughly a third of this audit.

**Testing — the suite tests a shadow system.** 124 passing tests, and they exercise functions the
running system does not call. There is also **zero coverage of any SQL, any query, or any
migration**, including the 1,462-line write path. No golden fixtures for CCN, MCN or the hurdle,
so a refactor that moves every score 3% is invisible. `nav_cad` — the number every position size
and the 30% bar depends on — has no test at all.

The cheapest real fix: run migrations against a throwaway Postgres in CI and assert the schema,
the guard triggers, and the write-path state machine. That is an afternoon and it would cover the
most dangerous untested surface in the repo.

**Reliability — the silent-failure surface is large.** A killed job reads green. A job that never
fires is invisible. One permanently-failing ticker pins the ingest amber forever, which blocks
every ticket. Zero earnings rows and zero swept names both report green. The most likely outcome
is not a crash — it is a week of confident briefs over stale data.

**Security — sound where it was designed, thin where it was inherited.** 018 is careful work.
Around it: eleven workflows holding the god-mode `DATABASE_URL` use floating action tags
(`checkout@v4`) while the only SHA-pinned workflow is the one with no secrets; `sslmode=require`
encrypts but does not verify the server, so it does not prevent an active MITM; the backup commits
a gzipped dump containing `runs.detail` — tracebacks and 1,400 raw characters of job stdout —
into the repo, where gitleaks cannot scan it; and `migrate.py` tracks applied migrations by
filename with no checksum, so the deployed security controls cannot be verified against the repo.

**DevOps — the crons are right, the recovery path is missing.** Schedules verify correctly against
§4.2 in both DST regimes at job start. But `migrate` has no heartbeat, no `DRY_RUN`, no concurrency
guard and no autopsy, and there is no restore tooling anywhere. A bad migration has no rollback.

**Database — the schema records state but does not defend it.** See Part 2 item 11.

### The honest bottom line

The *thinking* in this system is good. The rules are correctly specified, the plan-to-code ledger
is a genuinely strong control now that it verifies itself, and the write path is careful work.

But **the system as it stands should not propose a trade with real money.** Not because any one
defect is fatal, but because four of them compound: no position carries a stop (item 6), the
entry ticket is double-size (item 2), the market gate is not checked (item 4), and a bad print
cannot be caught (items 7, 8). That combination is how an account gets hurt.

The good news is that the expensive work — specifying the rules, testing them, building the
ledger — is done. What remains is mostly *connecting* it, and the ledger now names every
disconnection precisely.

## Suggested order

1. **Wire `policy` and delete the inline copies.** Closes items 1, 2, 3, the blackout duplication,
   and roughly a third of the audit. The ledger's `wired` count is the progress bar.
2. **Write `book.sleeve`** (item 6) — without it no stop machinery runs at all.
3. **Gate and approval checks in the ticket path** (items 4, 5).
4. **Bulk ingest + corporate-action refresh + price quarantine** (items 7, 8, 10).
5. **Heartbeat honesty**: treat `'running'` as stale, detect jobs that never fired (item 9).
6. **Schema defences**: CHECK constraints, `security_invoker` on the views, numeric for money.
7. **Migrations tested against a real Postgres in CI.**
8. **Re-run the backtest** once §3.2 is actually what runs — the current results describe a
   different system.

Items awaiting a ruling from Zak are in `docs/open-questions.md` and are not repeated here.
