## The finding to rule on before the memos

**Three of the seven top-ranked names have no measurable compounding engine at all**, and one of
them is now the highest CCN in the entire bench.

| Ticker | CCN | Engine | Cash conv | Size pct | What is carrying the score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FVRR | **97.4** | dropped | 6.24 | 98 | cash conversion + smallness, nothing else |
| YELP | 82.8 | dropped | 2.25 | 79 | cash conversion + smallness |
| VEON | 76.4 | dropped | 3.87 | 60 | cash conversion + smallness |

§3.3's data-confidence path says drop the component and renormalize, and the builder's floor
requires two components with at least one business measure — cash conversion qualifies. Both rules
are being followed exactly. The result is that **a company with no measurable compounding engine can
top a bench built to rank compounders**, which is close to a contradiction in terms. Fiverr at
CCN 97.4 has a 6% ROIC, zero measured reinvestment and a $330M market cap.

This is `learnings.md` #14 in a new costume: the first bench was topped by a $4 microcap on
smallness alone, that was fixed with the two-component floor, and the same pathology has returned
one component over. **My recommendation is a plan edit, not a code change:** the engine is the
component §3.1 calls "the compounding engine", and a name that cannot produce one should be
ineligible for the bench rather than ranked at the top of it. That is your call under the freeze
protocol, and it is the single highest-leverage line in this document.

## C2 memos

### PASS — recommended

**DOCS · Doximity · Health Information Services · CCN 72.0 · $3.9B · 75% below hurdle**
The professional network for US physicians, monetised by selling pharma and health-system access to
that audience. *Does scale strengthen it?* Yes, structurally: roughly 80% of US doctors are on it,
and a network at that penetration is the one asset a pharma marketer cannot route around. Each new
physician makes the platform more valuable to advertisers, and each advertiser dollar funds more
clinical tooling that keeps physicians there. *Gains shared to widen the moat?* Partly — the
clinical tools (telehealth, faxing, scheduling) are given away free to hold the audience, which is
the right shape of moat-widening even if it is not price-led. *Where does the next dollar go?* Into
product for a network it already owns, at a 97% ROIC on a tiny invested-capital base — the
reinvestment rate is genuinely low (15%) because there is little to reinvest *in*, which caps
compounding by internal growth but makes the cash real.
Proxies: gross margin ~90% and stable · share of physician audience saturated, share of pharma
budget rising · incremental margin high · revenue per employee strong.
⚠️ Serial-acquirer flag: goodwill jumped >25%. Small tuck-ins, worth a question but not a veto.
**PASS · confidence high.** The best business on this list.

**ADSK · Autodesk · Software – Application · CCN 61.1 · $49.6B · 47% below hurdle**
Design software with near-monopoly positions in architecture, engineering and construction. *Does
scale strengthen it?* Yes, through the most durable mechanism in software: file formats and trained
labour. A firm's drawings, its subcontractors' drawings and every graduate it hires are all in
Autodesk's formats. *Gains shared?* Yes — the subscription transition cut per-seat cost while
raising lifetime value, and the education programme gives the product away to the next generation of
users. *Next dollar?* Into cloud platform (Forma, Fusion) that raises switching costs further, at a
36% ROIC and 40% reinvestment — a real engine, cross-check clean.
Proxies: gross margin ~92% stable · share stable-to-rising in AEC · incremental margin high ·
revenue per employee rising through the cloud transition.
**PASS · confidence high.** The lowest CCN of the three passes, because size scores 12 — the score
penalises it for being large, not for being worse.

**TTD · The Trade Desk · Advertising Agencies · CCN 71.6 · $9.0B · 76% below hurdle**
The largest independent demand-side platform for programmatic advertising. *Does scale strengthen
it?* Yes — more spend means better bidding data, and independence from any media owner is a
structural position Amazon and Google cannot occupy. *Gains shared?* Yes, deliberately: take rate
has been held flat for years while volume grew, and UID2 was given to the industry rather than
licensed. *Next dollar?* Into CTV inventory relationships and identity, at 25% ROIC and 67%
reinvestment — the highest reinvestment rate on this list, which is what a compounding engine
actually looks like.
Proxies: gross margin ~80% stable · share losing ground to Amazon DSP — the live risk · incremental
margin high · revenue per employee high.
**PASS · confidence medium.** Honest caveat: this name is the compounder backtest's single loser
(−23% in 107 days), and a 76% discount to hurdle means the market disagrees with this memo
violently. The business quality is real; the competitive question is genuinely open.

### FAIL — recommended

**FVRR · Fiverr · CCN 97.4 · $330M · 46% below hurdle.** A freelance-services marketplace. Scale
helps a two-sided market, but the moat is thin — sellers and buyers multi-home to Upwork trivially —
and generative AI directly substitutes for the largest categories on the platform (copywriting,
graphic design, translation). The next dollar has nowhere productive to go: measured reinvestment is
zero and ROIC is 6%. **FAIL · confidence high.** The CCN is an artifact, per the finding above.

**YELP · CCN 82.8 · $1.5B · 18% below hurdle.** Local reviews and advertising. The review corpus is
a genuine asset, but Google has been disintermediating it for a decade and revenue growth has been
low single digits — which is exactly why the engine failed its cross-check. A business whose
computed engine says 16% while its revenue says otherwise is not compounding; it is generating cash
from a stable, slowly-eroding position. **FAIL · confidence high.**

**VEON · CCN 76.4 · $3.5B · 0.3% below hurdle.** Emerging-market telecom across Ukraine, Pakistan,
Bangladesh and Kazakhstan. Capital-intensive, currency-exposed, and carrying geopolitical risk that
cannot be underwritten at any multiple. Cash conversion of 3.87 reflects depreciation of a
past capital cycle, not a compounding engine. **FAIL · confidence high.**

**CXM · Sprinklr · CCN 81.2 · $1.5B · 66% below hurdle.** Customer-experience management suite.
Cross-check clean and ROIC 34%, so the engine is real — but the competitive position is not: it
sells into a crowded field against Salesforce, Adobe and Qualtrics, growth has decelerated sharply,
and switching costs at the mid-market end are low. Scale does not obviously strengthen it.
**FAIL · confidence medium.** The one I would most readily revisit if you disagree.

**EXEL · Exelixis · CCN 55.4 · $14.1B · 14% below hurdle.** Oncology, concentrated in cabozantinib
with patent exposure toward 2030. Scale does not compound in single-asset biotech; the next dollar
goes into clinical trials with binary outcomes, which is the opposite of a reinvestment runway.
**FAIL · confidence high.**

**INCY · Incyte · CCN 52.8 · $24.9B · 31% below hurdle.** Same shape, more acute: Jakafi
concentration with 2028 expiry. **FAIL · confidence high.**

**LULU · Lululemon · CCN 48.5 · $13.6B · 28% below hurdle.** Was a textbook compounder. Cash
conversion is now 0.84 — below one, meaning reported profit is not arriving as cash — Americas
growth has stalled, and Alo and Vuori are taking the premium end. The thesis that made it great is
the one now in question. **FAIL · confidence medium.**

**36% of NAV**, inside the 60% compounder ceiling. They fall in three different industry groups
(Health Information Services, Software – Application, Advertising Agencies), so §2.2's two-per-group
cap does not bind. Themes are assigned when the tickets are written; on my read DOCS and ADSK are
independent, and TTD shares no macro driver with either.

Also on the table and not part of these memos: §6 Step 5 is ruled — the levered layer funds
`VXC.TO`, which implies selling CNQ.TO (142 shares, NONREG) and deploying the LOC into the ETF.
That executes in Phase L once `phase0.py` is rebuilt onto the arming layer.
