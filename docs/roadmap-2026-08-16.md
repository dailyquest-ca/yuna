### Every red and amber on the board, and what each one is

- **`score` amber — `engine NAV unknown`.** The root blocker, and it is Zak's. Buys are written
  **unsized** and §4.3 forbids executing them. One row: `config.engine_nav`.
- **`check` red — freshness.** A consequence of the amber above, not a second fault. §4.4: any red
  holds the buys, and the buys are unexecutable anyway. It clears when the NAV lands.
- **`check` amber — screen 2333 against a band of [2336, 2336].** The band is computed from **one**
  prior session, so it is a point and any movement at all trips it. It widens as sessions
  accumulate; amber does not hold buys. Left alone rather than given an invented tolerance.
- **`compose` amber — "the brief ships the sheet with its buys held (§4.4, §5.4)".** Correct
  behaviour, printed. A red night is exactly the night the message has to go out.

### Waiting on a ruling (§0.3)

**~~Does the engine read the account or the sleeve?~~ Ruled 2026-08-19.** Zak: *"The sleeve is the
purpose of the money. We just set the boundaries as the account for simplicity but one day some of
the RRSP may be used for Momentum and maybe some of the TFSA will be used for something else."*

The account filter STAYS — his ruling makes it a deliberate simplification rather than a design —
and the TFSA's three positions were relabelled `momentum` to match §2.1 and their actual purpose:
AXTI and MU are pre-seed buys of momentum names, and the SPMO 810 is the §6.1(3) bridge, momentum
capital parked while it waits to seed. RRSP stays `reserve`, NONREG stays `levered`.

`desk.sleeve_divergence` now reads clean, and that is the point of having done it: the first time
purpose and wrapper disagree it will be a signal rather than three rows of pre-existing noise. That
is the day the account filter has to become a sleeve filter, and it will say so.

**~~The seed would fill three slots, not five.~~ Ruled 2026-08-19.** Zak: *"The strategy should
auto suggest the right thing... If it suggests leaving anything in SPMO there is a problem."* So it
does: under seed conditions (gate ON, shadow passed, park held) a kept top-12 name below its weight
gets a `top_up` buy to NAV/5, funded by the park sell on the same sheet — nothing stays in SPMO.

The scoping is what keeps it §3.5-clean with no invented constant: top-ups exist only while the park
can pay for them, so post-seed (park empty) a name bought at weight is never bought again and NAV
growth can never start pyramiding winners. Off-conditions the shortfall is still reported.

## After the seed

**Delete the retired engine.** §6.3 retires the legacy jobs *from the schedule*, and that is what
has been done — `score.py`, `check.py`, `compose.py`, `fills.py`, `signals.py`, `arming.py`,
`rank.py`, `fundamentals.py`, `funnel.py`, `phase0.py` are all still on disk, dispatchable, and
guarded against creeping back into the chain by `test_workflows_parse.py`. Deleting six thousand
lines in the same change that repointed production would have risked a night with no desk. Once
the shadow has passed and the engine has a live record, the deletion is its own change with its own
review.

**§2.5's review checkpoint.** At the first completed gate cycle (ON→OFF→ON) **or** 12 months of
live record, whichever comes first: one full-structure review in one sitting. The Saturday letter
counts gate flips over the whole record for exactly this reason — a rolling window would reset the
thing the checkpoint is waiting for.
