**Issued:** 2026-08-14 · **Author:** Zak · **Approver:** Zak (§4.5)

Filed verbatim as issued. Two notes on how it lands in this repo:

**Numbering.** An earlier file in this session also carries A6 —

**Amendment, approved by Zak 2026-08-14 before any cell ran.** §4's falsifier is a *start-offset*
spread test. That is the same axis mistaken for a phase test on the bi-monthly book: a start
offset drops the opening trades but does not change *which* observations the rule acts on. For a
state-door there may genuinely be no calendar phase to shift — but that is §0's claim under test,
so the test must not assume it. A second falsifier is added:

> **Rank-lag falsifier.** Run the center reading the rank and the base state on session *i − k*
> while executing on session *i*, for k ∈ {1, 2, 5}. A door responding to a slow state should
> barely notice being read a day or a week late. A result that moves materially was riding a
> specific-day effect, which is precisely the failure a start-offset test cannot see.

Both interpretation calls the specification required are pinned in `src/concentrated.py` and
tested:

* **Clustering method (§2 requires one pre-registered):** single-linkage at ρ > 0.70 over 126
  sessions. Chosen because it merges readily and therefore caps more aggressively than
  complete-linkage — a rider meant to prevent a 1.84-effective-bet book should err toward calling
  names related rather than unrelated.
* **Effective-bets floor:** binds from the fifth name onward. A three-name book cannot have five
  effective bets under any correlation structure, so a literal reading refuses every entry and
  the book never fills. Below five names only the cluster cap applies. Where correlation cannot
  be measured the rider abstains and logs, rather than blocking — the alternative is a
  liquidity-and-history filter masquerading as a diversification rule.

## 0 · The design thesis (why this arm exists)

The frequency axis proved two things at once: raw return rises all the way to daily (the 12-1÷vol signal is real and decays fast) and Sharpe peaks at bi-monthly (calendar turnover eats the freshness faster than it earns past that point). The daily cell's flaw is structural: it **welds the observation clock to the transaction clock** — re-ranking daily *and* forcing the book to equal the top-8 daily, which re-trades rank flicker at the margin of a slow signal ($416k of spreads, −56% expected DD, Sharpe below the ETF).

A6 separates the clocks. **Observe every session; transact only on gates.** Entries via a state-door (the strongest *qualifying* name when a slot frees — not "whatever broke out today," the design flaw that produced §10's 1.54%). Exits via the trail (primary) or a rank hysteresis band (a name is held until it *travels*, not until it flickers). No calendar exists in the rule, so rebalance-timing luck dies by construction rather than by tranching.

Pre-registered predictions: turnover **< 100 names/yr** at center; mean CAGR **≥ the bi-monthly candidate's 33.37%**; start-offset spread **≤ 3 points** by construction; bootstrap-median DD **≤ −40%** required, ≤ −35% aspiration.

## 1 · A6 center specification

**Universe & rank:** top-500 US names by point-in-time dollar volume, price ≥ $5; rank = 12-1 momentum ÷ realized volatility.

**Book:** N = **12**. Entry size = equity/12, funded from park. Weights drift; **no conformance trading, no trims**. Idle capital parks in **SPMO**; if no qualifying candidate exists when a slot frees, the slot stays parked — never force an entry.

**Observation clock:** every session. **Transaction clock:** gates only, all executions at next morning's open.

**Entry gate (the state-door).** A slot is free → buy the **highest-ranked** name satisfying: (1) rank ≤ **15**; (2) valid-base state — close within **10%** of its 252-session high close, close > 50-day SMA, and 50-day SMA today > 50-day SMA ten sessions ago; (3) the correlation rider. If the top qualifier fails the rider, step down the rank to the next qualifier.

**Exit gates (any one fires; next-open execution):** (1) **Trail** — §3.2 as written. (2) **Rank band** — rank > **40** at the nightly observation. Between 15 and 40, flicker generates zero trades.

**Costs:** the sourced curve, charged on every leg including park round-trips.

## 2 · Mandatory rider — effective-bets floor at formation

At every entry the resulting book must satisfy **effective bets ≥ 5** (§2.2 formula, trailing 126 sessions) and **≤ 2 names per correlation cluster** (pairwise ρ > 0.7). Gates **formation only — never forces exits**; continuous effective bets is *reported*, not enforced.

## 4 · Falsifiers and pass bars

- **Start-offset test:** center at 5 offsets {0, +10, +21, +42, +63 sessions}. **Kill: spread > 6 points.**
- **Rank-lag test (amendment):** k ∈ {1, 2, 5}. Same kill bar.
- **Livability:** report names/yr and minutes/week. **Flag any cell > 2 trades/week regardless of CAGR.**
- **§2.5 in full, SPMO the benchmark:** bootstrap-median CAGR > SPMO ex-top-3, DSR ≥ 0.95 at the live ledger count, OOS cut, bootstrap-median DD ≤ −40%.
- **Kill:** bootstrap-median < SPMO ⇒ hold-the-ETF stands; turnover > 150/yr ⇒ redesign, don't tune.

# Results

Everything below was measured, not estimated. The centre is **run 318** (`a6_floor0`), window

## 8 · Q-queries — answered

### Q1 · Deployed-vs-park attribution — **answered, and it was a real gap**

The equity ledger could not answer this question when the WO was written: it stored NAV and
nothing else, so "26% CAGR" could not be split into what the book earned and what the park earned.
The row is now five columns — date, NAV, park mark, deployed fraction, position count — and every
cell writes them.

For the centre: **84.1% mean deployed, 97.7% median, 10.12 mean positions**, 18 sessions out of
2260 fully parked, minimum deployment 0.0%. The book is not a market-timer in disguise. It is
invested essentially always, and the gap between the 84.1% mean and the 97.7% median is the tail of
partial-fill sessions immediately after a cluster of stops, not a cash-timing decision.

### Q2 · Earnings-gap share of the 20 worst trades — **zero. The tail is thematic, not idiosyncratic**

Joined against the `earnings` table (157,927 rows, 4,845 tickers, 2015-01 → 2027-02, so the whole
window is covered). A trade counts as an earnings exit if a report for that ticker is dated within
the three days ending on the exit date — generous enough to catch Friday-after-close into a Monday
exit.

| cohort | n | mean P&L | exited on an earnings gap | held through a report |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 worst | 20 | −21.07% | **0 (0.0%)** | 2 |
| 50 worst | 50 | −17.81% | 3 (6.0%) | 9 |
| all losers | 492 | −9.07% | 28 (5.7%) | 118 |
| all trades | 949 | +2.77% | 57 (6.0%) | 350 |

**The worst 20 contain no earnings exits at all, against a 6.0% base rate.** What they contain
instead is six macro dates:

| exit date | names | what it was |
|---|---|---|
| 2020-03-12 | NEE | the COVID crash |
| 2020-11-10 | QDEL, PTON | the vaccine Monday — the COVID-beneficiary unwind |
| 2021-02-23 | MARA, RIOT, NIO | the crypto/EV growth unwind |
| 2021-03-04 | DQ | the rate-shock leg of the same unwind |
| 2021-04-21 | FUTU | the China-ADR leg |
| 2025-04-04 / 04-07 | SE, SPOT, TRGP | the tariff crash |

Nine of the twenty worst trades fall on six dates. **The tail is correlated, and it is thematic.**
That is the risk this book actually carries — the one the §2 rider is aimed at — and it is not the
risk an earnings filter would address.

The second column is the reason no gate gets legislated:

| | n | mean P&L | win rate | best | worst | mean bars |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| held through a report | 350 | **+8.14%** | 66.3% | +115.0% | −18.6% | 40.5 |
| never saw one | 599 | −0.37% | 37.4% | +62.9% | −28.4% | 14.5 |

Read this with care: most of that gap is survivorship on duration — a trade has to live ~40 sessions
to meet a report at all, and a trade that lives that long is by construction one the trail did not
stop. It is **not** evidence that earnings exposure pays. But it is decisive against the gate: an
earnings-avoidance rule would sell into the cohort that contains every large winner, to avoid a risk
that produced none of the twenty worst losses. **Measured, no gate. §0's instinct — drift is partly
where momentum pays — is not contradicted by anything here.**

### Q3 · Jackknife benchmark identity — **was wrong, fixed at source**

`BENCH` was `VOO.US`, so every §2.5 verdict in the A4 and A5 families was scored against
15.45% / +262.7% instead of SPMO's 21.12% / +453.9%. Every `proven` verdict issued before the fix
cleared the wrong bar. The run now writes `benchmark: SPMO.US` into `params` and the scorer reads
the park ticker; `regime_source` stays `VOO.US`, which is a different job (the broad-market regime
read) and is correctly named.

**Data note found while verifying:** SPMO's stored tape has 26 missing sessions, all of them between
2017-08-18 and 2017-11-24 — the ETF's listing ramp, when it barely traded. Nothing after 2017-11-24
is missing. The benchmark comparison is therefore honest from 2017-11-25 onward and slightly
coarse for its first three months. Not material at nine years; logged rather than fixed.

### Q4 · Ledger consistency — **clean**

Run 318: 949 trades, **0 rows with NULL P&L, 0 open legs**. Every position that was still held at
the last bar is closed at that bar's mark and written as a leg, so the jackknife sees the whole
book rather than only the part that stopped out. The `exits` census is `trail_stop: 947,
open_at_end: 2` — **every exit in the run is §3.2's own trail.** No exit reason the plan does not
name.

Two things must be said plainly about the DSR.

**First, it fails, and the failure is not a technicality.** At 173 logged trials the deflation asks
the observed per-session Sharpe of 0.0617 to clear an expected-best-under-the-null of 0.0455; it
clears it by z = 0.75, which is a 77th-percentile result where the bar wants the 95th. Read
literally: *having searched this hard, a Sharpe this good would turn up by chance roughly one time
in four.*

**Second, the trial count is mine, not the strategy's.** Every falsifier cell in the table above
also enters the ledger and pushes the bar up. That is the statistic working correctly — a
falsification run is still a look at the data — but it means the number will not improve by running
more cells, only by out-of-sample evidence. The 2003–2016 backfill is the instrument that moves it;
running further variants moves it the wrong way.

The honest verdict is neither "A6 works" nor "A6 failed": **A6 is the first arm in this programme to
survive its own falsifiers on a filled book, and it remains formally `unproven` under a
search-corrected bar.**

### Amendment note — the effective-bets floor as written cannot bind

§2's floor ("effective bets ≥ 5 at every entry") was implemented first as "binds from the fifth name
onward". That reading is arithmetically impossible: *k* equally-weighted names have
`k / (1 + (k−1)ρ̄)` effective bets, which reaches 5 at k = 5 only when ρ̄ = 0. The book capped at
**3.81 names and 32.6% deployed** — the arm ran crippled, and `a6_floor4` (277 trades) is what that
looks like. `a6_floor0` runs the cluster cap alone and is the cell reported above. **The floor needs
a ruling from Zak before it can be re-enabled** — either a reachable level, or a rule that scales
with book size.
