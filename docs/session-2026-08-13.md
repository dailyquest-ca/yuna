## 1 · The A2 conformance failure was not where the handoff pointed

The handoff said "narrowed to coverage on two earnings clauses." The stored tables say otherwise:
on run 56, M4 coverage reads 0.982 and the blackout 0.948 — both healthy. The failing clause on
all three A2 runs is **Entry — GTC stop-limit, pivot / pivot+2%**, with `violations` equal to the
run's entire trade count (1,352 / 1,024 / 1,014).

The mechanism: the day loop counted every non-base, non-recovery entry in `reentries`, and the
Entry clause's violation waiver knew only the two non-base doors earlier variants had declared —
X1's `reentry_window` and C1's `screen`. A2's `entry_new_high` door was never taught to the
table, so the arm's own entry rule was reported as a thousand undeclared violations of a §3.2
clause about re-entry.

Fixed in the engine: `new_high` entries are counted at their own door (`conf.new_high_entries`),
the Entry clause declares them (`new_highs`), and the waiver excuses them only when the
hypothesis actually sets `entry_new_high` — an undeclared new-high entry still fails, which is
the guard working. Runs 54–56 keep their recorded `false`; the ledger is history, and all three
were invalid as specified anyway.

## 2 · P1 was only half built, and the A2 runs prove it

Runs 54, 55 and 56 **share param_hash `dd8735ff2ac4dace`** while measuring three different

54/55/56 got called one experiment.

## 3 · The capture audit is `src/capture_audit.py`, and every run gets it

The standing rule, built as specified — three parts:

1. **The net** — every push the window offered vs what the arm entered. A push is a close above
   the prior 252 own closes (`new_high_breakout`'s own test) that runs **+50% before any close
   gives the level back**; a breakout that returns to its level first is a failed breakout, in
   neither column. Episodes chain after completion, so a decade-long trend counts once per +50%
   leg rather than once per new-high day — this subsumes the handoff's "name-months" bucketing
   and is recorded as the one deviation from its phrasing. Eligibility at the breakout is
   `rank()`'s own L0 census test (≥210 bars in the last 252 union sessions, raw ≥ $5,
   50-session median dollar volume ≥ $10M): a push the census could never admit is not a
   catchable push. No M2, no MCN, no gate — the net exists to measure the filters too.
2. **The ride** — for each caught push, the positions' realized P&L against holding the first
   entry to the +50% mark. Clipped-after-three-days reads as a small share; entered-and-held
   reads near 1.
3. **The junk** — positions overlapping no push of their name, with their P&L.

Writes `stats.capture_audit` on the run row; crashes record themselves there before re-raising
(Actions logs are unreachable — learnings #13). Wired into `backtest.yml` after the conformance
report, under `always()` — a run that failed conformance needs its capture on the table more,
not less. Unit-tested on hand-built tapes (`tests/test_capture_audit.py`): the chaining, the
failed-breakout rule, the unresolved-race rule, the $5-raw and ADDV floors, and all three parts'
arithmetic.

## 4 · §2.5 has a driver: `src/finding.py`

`bars.py` promised "the driver that reads a run and writes a verdict lives elsewhere" — it now
exists. Per run: both cuts (full window + the Aug-2025 OOS cut, sessions from 2025-08-01);
winner-exclusion jackknife at account level (arithmetic, flagged — it flatters, so only a FAIL
is conclusive); the 63-session circular block bootstrap at seed 0; the deflated Sharpe against
the logged E-series trial count; and the 90/10 chassis counterfactual, computed exactly as the
construction that reproduces the recorded +222.10% on run 53's window (validated to the basis
point before use).

Two interpretations are recorded rather than assumed:

- **Trials (forward-only, per Zak's 2026-08-12 ruling):** runs carrying a `param_hash`,
  deduplicated by (param_hash, code_stamp), with unstamped runs counting singly — §2 above is
  why sameness cannot be assumed where the code was never stamped. At E1's scoring time the
  ledger holds trials 54, 55, 56 + the E1 run itself; `MIN_TRIALS = 50` floors the deflation.
- **DSR frequency:** computed entirely in per-session terms — Sharpe and skew/kurtosis from the
  same daily return series. The work order's words are "the trade distribution's skew/kurtosis";
  the Bailey–López de Prado expression is defined on the return series the Sharpe is measured
  on, and mixing a daily Sharpe with per-trade moments would put two frequencies in one formula.

A full-window pass whose OOS bootstrap-median loses to the benchmark is demoted to unproven with
the reason on the record — §2.5(a) makes the OOS cut part of the definition, not decoration.
Writes `stats.bars_25`; wired into `backtest.yml` beside the audit; `dd_bar` joined the dispatch
inputs so E3 cells can declare their −0.34 bar at dispatch time.

## 9 · The mission, second wave — and a reproduction failure that outranks it

Runs 67-71 continued the ladder. Three engine defects were found and fixed, one measured lever
was banked, and the wave ended by exposing something more important than any of them.

### The three defects, each found by the one before it

All the same shape — **an arm's spec replaces §3.2's machinery and the engine keeps running it**:

1. **The stop was anchored on the fill, not the level.** Pushes open +1.28% above the level they
   cleared (median +0.39%); failures open flat. A3 recorded `pivot = fill = the next open` and
   stopped on a close below it — a breakeven stop on day one, run 54's defect wearing A3's name.
2. **The door had no limit.** Run 67's hero gapped down 25% from its signal close and the engine
   *bought it at the collapsed open*. §3.2's base door has always refused a fill past its
   ceiling; the new-high door now rests the stop-limit it always claimed to be.
3. **The law's stop ladder ran behind every A3 position.** The bypass keyed on `chandelier_mult`
   alone, so an arm with a level stop and no trail in its spec was silently carrying §3.2's 10%
   trail from +15% — clipping every winner 10% off its high. **This was the whole ride gap.**

## 10 · The bug, found and fixed — and the best result the programme has produced

§9's "reproduction failure" was not tape drift. It was a real bug, and finding it changed the
standing of everything.

**What actually happened.** Runs 46–53 executed *before* commit `444413f`, on flat 5/15bps costs
and a park rebalanced **daily**. Everything since uses §2.2's curve and §2.1's **banded** true-up.
The stored params prove it: run 53 carries `half_spread_bps {deep 5, thin 15}` and `park: null`;
run 71 carries the curve and `park {band 0.05, check_every 5}`.

**The bug the band exposed.** The `a1v` preset states the rule in as many words — *"an entry that
cannot be funded from cash sells the index to fund itself. Without that, the 10% floor would
quietly starve the sleeve"* — and the entry path simply **skipped** any position cash could not
cover. `park_sell` was wired only into the periodic true-up and the final liquidation. Under the
old daily rebalance cash returned to exactly the target every session, so a first tranche needing
~8.3% of NAV always fit. Under the band, cash drifts toward 5% and the entry vanished in silence.

**The measured cost was the entire headline:**

**§9's withdrawal of E1 is itself withdrawn.** Run 57 excluded MU deliberately and run 71 starved

why the delta looked like 0.3 points. Micron is worth roughly five CAGR points, as originally

sleeve's breadth problem is still the open question.

## 11 · The park-tilt ladder, and the control that ends the argument

**Performance first, measured rather than guessed.** The backtest job averages 436s against
push_study's 101s and blend's 4s, and `rank()` already runs weekly. The instrumentation now says
where it goes: **load 141.6s, simulate ~260s per cell.** Two structural costs were wasting most
of the wall clock, and one was self-inflicted:

- the workflow's concurrency group keyed only on (ref, research) with `cancel-in-progress`, so two
  engine dispatches on the same branch **cancelled each other** — cells could not overlap at all;
- every dispatch re-pulled the whole tape (~2.2M rows), so an N-cell ladder paid N loads and N
  times the Supabase egress.

Both fixed: the group now carries the hypothesis set, and `HYPOTHESES` prices a ladder against
**one** load, writing each cell as its own run row and scoring every one. Five cells then ran as
two concurrent sweeps in the wall-clock of about one old serial run.

### The ladder — the park's vehicle is the whole story

Monotone, and it improves the drawdown at every step. a1v_b100 also **passes the ex-top-3 clause**

**Two clean negatives, both cheap:**

### The control that ends the argument

|---|---|---|

**The momentum sleeve adds 0.05 CAGR points over simply holding the ETF.** The whole 21% is
SPMO. What the sleeve buys is risk, not return: 3.8 points of drawdown (−27.2% against −31.0%),

**§2.4.1's pre-commitment fires on this number.** The work order wrote it before any of this was
measured: *"in-house edge over SPMO < ~2 pts/yr net (full + OOS) ⇒ the momentum-beta allocation is
the ETF."* The measured edge is 0.05 points. That is the honest terminus the E-series named for
itself — **hold the ETF** — and it arrives with the drawdown footnote attached rather than as a
defeat: the chassis (park + gate + sleeve) does measurably reduce the tail.

**On the 30% mission.** The best the evidence supports is ~21%/yr with a −27% drawdown, and
essentially all of it is buyable in one ticker. Nothing in nine years of this tape, across
seventeen configurations, produced a single-name overlay worth more than noise on top of that.
That is not the answer the mission wanted, but it is the answer the data gives, and it is worth
more than a fitted one.

**What remains genuinely open** (and would be the next work, not more variants of the same):
the bear gate (E-final) — SPMO has no history before 2015-10, so a 21% CAGR measured in an
uninterrupted bull market is exactly the number a bear gate exists to interrogate; §2.4.2's
concentration review, which SPMO in any production role triggers; and the drawdown finding, which
is the one thing the sleeve demonstrably earns.

## 12 · WO-A4 — the winner-catcher, tested on Zak's own specification

Zak's redirect was correct about the work: the E-series tested **breadth** arms (thirty slots,
thousands of trades) and index blends, and neither is the thesis. The thesis is *"picking out the
winners... catching all of the +100% within a year... 10-15 names a year because we want to work
our day job."* That is a **precision** arm, and it had never been run.

**The population is real.** 50–282 liquid US names double in a given year (median ~140 of roughly
3,000 eligible — a ~5% base rate). Catching a dozen of them is not a fantasy; it is a selection
problem with a known denominator.

`src/concentrated.py` implements exactly that idea and **nothing else** — no base, no pivot, no
MCN, no confirmation machine, no trim ladder. Rank the liquid universe by 12-1 momentum (the
academic standard and SPMO's own published method, volatility-adjusted as SPMO does), hold the
top N equal-weighted, change the book on a slow clock, park the remainder in SPMO. Two bugs were
caught by its own tests before any result was believed: N equal slices of NAV cannot all be
funded once each pays a spread (the last name silently went unbought), and a holding that stops
printing was dropped from the mark — **run 52's defect, reappearing in code written today**, and
the tell was a −100.0% drawdown on a long-only book.

### The grid, both pools

| Cell | Pool | Sleeve | CAGR | Max DD | OOS | Entries/yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SPMO alone** | — | — | **21.12%** | **−30.95%** | — | 0 |
| lg12_semi_third | top-500 | 30% | 20.50% | −30.95% | +21.7% | 15.5 |
| n12_semi_third | all | 30% | 20.07% | −30.98% | — | 15.6 |
| n12_semi_half | all | 50% | 19.40% | −36.32% | — | 15.6 |
| lg12_semi | top-500 | 100% | 17.65% | −61.28% | −10.7% | 15.5 |
| n12_semi | all | 100% | 16.66% | −56.52% | — | 15.6 |
| lg12_annual | top-500 | 100% | 12.61% | −53.76% | −12.1% | 4.1 |
| n12_quarterly | all | 100% | 5.23% | −63.47% | — | 32.3 |

**Read it in one line: the more of the account left in the ETF, the better it did — on return
AND on drawdown.** Every full-sleeve cell drew down 54–63% and every one has a *negative*
out-of-sample cut. The best concentrated cell (20.50%) is 70% SPMO already, and still lands 0.6
points under simply holding SPMO.

Three clean mechanism findings, each cheap and each reusable:

- **The volatility adjustment is load-bearing.** Raw 12-1 returns 10.35% with a −94.28%
  drawdown; the same rank divided by realized volatility returns 16.66%. SPMO's adjustment is not
  cosmetic.
- **Turnover destroys it.** Quarterly rebalancing returns 5.23% against semi-annual's 16.66% on
  otherwise identical rules. The slow clock the day-job constraint demands is also the profitable
  one — the constraint and the edge point the same way.
- **Large caps did not rescue concentration.** Ranking inside the 500 most-traded names (SPMO's
  own neighbourhood, and where MRVL/GOOGL/SQ/MU live) lifted return by one point and made the
  drawdown four points *worse*.

### What this actually answers

The question was "how could we have caught them during their uptrend." Across today's evidence
the catching was never the hard part — A3 entered 39% of every push on the tape. The hard part is
that **concentration raises idiosyncratic risk faster than it raises expected return**: twelve
names lose 60% in a drawdown where a hundred lose 31%, and the twelve do not compound faster to
pay for it.

One honest limit on all of this: a backtest can only test *rules*. "Pick MRVL, GOOGL, SQ and MU"
as a systematic rule is 12-1 momentum, and that is what was measured. It cannot measure judgment
applied to those names, and it should not pretend to.

## 13 · Three defects in one file, and every A4 number before this section is withdrawn

§12's conclusions were measured on a grid with three bugs in it. All three were found by asking
the trade ledger questions rather than by reading its reports, and the largest one had been
producing the headline results all along.

### The grid spent half of every other year in SPMO

`build_grid` took its session list from the tape's own union of dates. Two dozen junk listings in
the store print on US market holidays — 26 names on 2018-01-01, 28 on 2026-01-01, and VOO on
neither — so New Year's Day was a session. `rebalance_dates` takes the first session of each
half-year, which landed exactly on it. On a day when nothing real prints, no name clears the $5
raw-price floor, so the rank came up **empty**: the book sold everything (selling carries the
last mark, correctly) and bought nothing (buying refuses a stale mark, also correctly). The cash
went to the park and stayed there until July.

It fired on 2018-01-01, 2019-01-01, 2020-01-01 and 2026-01-01, and it explains every §12 result
that did not add up:

- every cell pinned to **-30.95% on 2020-03-23** — SPMO's own drawdown to the basis point —
  because on that session the concentrated book held no stocks at all
- the 30%-sleeve cells looked strong for a reason that had nothing to do with the sleeve
- the ledger showed NVDA and MU held July-to-January and never otherwise, which read like a
  finding about the clock and was an artefact of the calendar

The market calendar is now the benchmark's own bars — an index ETF prints on every real session
and no holiday — and `build_grid` refuses to build without one. Two health counters make the
shape impossible to hide again: `stale_skips` counts every buy refused on a stale mark, and
`empty_rebalances` names every rebalance that ended holding nothing, by either route.

### A third of a twelve-name book was one stock

On 2021-01-04 every cell held BBBY.US, BBBY_old.US, OSTK.US **and** BYON.US — four slots, all
Overstock, through the meme window those slots returned +82.5% in. On 2022-01-03 the same books
held MMC.US and MRSH.US, which is Marsh McLennan twice. When a listing changes symbol the vendor
back-fills the new symbol and keeps the old one carrying the same series; both sit in `universe`
as active stocks with closes agreeing to the cent.

Migration 047 excluded 35 such lines on a closes signature and **missed the two that mattered**,
for two reasons worth keeping: it built each signature from the dates a line *has*, so lines with
different coverage never group (SQ.US ends 2025-01-21, XYZ.US runs to today — seven sampled
closes against eight, and the pair sailed through despite 2,122 shared sessions with 2,122
identical closes), and it compared closes when 045 had already recorded that returns are the
stronger test. 048 signs on daily returns over a fixed, padded date vector; SQ.US is excluded on
2,121 of 2,121 identical returns.

047 also came within one row of deleting a company: it excluded SGH.US as a duplicate of
PENG_old.US, which an earlier migration had itself excluded in favour of SGH.US. The group
survived only because the live line, PENG.US, sits outside it. 048 chooses keepers only from
lines not already excluded.

**Recorded residue.** BBBY_old.US and BBBY.US share 2,274 sessions and agree on 86.7% of closes
— plainly one listing, plainly below any threshold defensible as a rule. It needs a
corporate-actions source rather than a tighter similarity test.

### The rebalance sized out of the cash it had just freed

`nav = cash`, on a line sitting under a block that had just liquidated everything unwanted — so
every new slice was sized out of an account missing all of its carried names. The new test
measures $115,598 invested out of $147,894: a cell labelled `sleeve=1.00` really running 0.78.
Smallest of the three in effect (~0.2 points of CAGR), and real.

### Two more, in the scoring rather than the simulation

`capture_audit.load_positions` coerced every leg's P&L with `float(pnl)` and the ledger stores
NULL for a leg still open at the window's end — so the audit died the moment it was pointed at an
arm that ends holding its book. Coercing to 0.0 would have been worse: the trade still being
ridden on the last session would have entered the ride arithmetic as one that kept none of its
move. And the euphoria test read a flat window as euphoric, because "> mean + 2 x 0" is true of
any uptick — a halted name came back from the halt on a 5% leash.

### What §12 said, and what it should have said

Every number in §12, and every A4 run from 95 to 164, measured a half-parked book holding
duplicate listings. The three findings that section drew — that large caps made concentration
worse, that turnover destroys it, and that the market gate does nothing — are **withdrawn**. The
first two reversed outright on the corrected grid.

### The large-cap pool is the biggest single lever, and §12 had its sign backwards

Full universe 27.31% → top 500 **36.02%** → top 250 **37.54%**, on otherwise identical rules.
Ranking 12-1 momentum across ~3,000 liquid names reaches into small and mid caps where the
signal is mostly volatility that mean-reverts; ranking inside the most-traded 250 is the
neighbourhood SPMO ranks in, and it is worth **ten points of CAGR**. Zak's instinct was right and
§12 said the opposite because it was measuring a half-parked book.

### The deflated Sharpe splits the grid where the CAGR column does not

41 trials in the ledger, trial-Sharpe spread 0.0131, expected max from selection alone 0.0298.
Against a 0.95 bar:

- **the trailed cells pass** — 0.987, 0.980, 0.966
- **the high-CAGR untrailed cells fail** — 0.945, 0.935, 0.928

That is the whole finding in one line: **the 37.5% is not distinguishable from what searching
twenty-two cells would produce by luck; the 31.75% is.** The untrailed cells buy their return
with variance, and variance is exactly what the deflation prices. Nothing in the grid reaches the
work order's aspiration of 0.9987 (t ≈ 3).

### The exit was always the missing piece, and it was already law

§3.2's ratchet — stop at entry -8%, armed at +15% from average cost, trailing 10% below the
highest close since entry, tightened to 5% when the close is 2sd above its own 50-day — applied
per name, every session, to a book that had none:

| | CAGR | maxDD | bootstrap median DD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| lg12_semi (no exit between rebalances) | 36.02% | -46.9% | -51.9% |
| lg12_semi_trail | 29.19% | -30.7% | -31.0% |

It gives up six points of return and cuts the *expected* drawdown by twenty-one. At eight names
it gives up almost nothing: **lg8_semi_trail, 31.75% at -31.2%, 16.7 new names a year** — SPMO's
drawdown with ten and a half points more return, and a trade count a person with a job can carry.

### Two risk layers that do not earn their place

- **The market gate** costs 6.2 points of CAGR (36.02% → 29.86%) to save 2.3 points of drawdown.
  It fires on the index's 200-day, which is not the series that forecasts a momentum crash.
- **The Barroso-Santa-Clara governor** was the hypothesis that the *strategy's own* volatility is
  that series. It is — but the trail already collects the same information per name and acts on
  it faster: with the trail on, adding the governor takes 29.19% → 23.00% for one point of
  drawdown, at more than double the turnover. **The trail dominates the governor.** Hypothesis
  raised, tested, rejected.

### The unglamorous lever nobody asked about

`n12_semi_raw` (raw 12-1) returns **14.86%**; `n12_semi` (12-1 divided by realized volatility)
returns **27.31%**. Dividing by the name's own volatility is worth twelve points — more than
concentration, more than the clock, more than the gate. It is also what SPMO's published
methodology does.

### What this does not establish

The window holds one bear market (2022) and no 2008. The OOS cut is a single year. Twenty-two
cells on one tape is a search, and the DSR prices it at 0.987 for the champion — good, not
settled. BBBY_old.US/BBBY.US still double-counts for four weeks in 2021. And a backtest tests
*rules*: "pick MRVL, GOOGL, SQ, MU" as a rule is 12-1 momentum inside a large-cap pool, which is
what was measured — it cannot measure judgment applied to those names and does not claim to.
