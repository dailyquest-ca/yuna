# WO-A8 — The five-name weekly book: what it is, and what makes its Sharpe move

**Issued:** 2026-08-14 · **Author:** Claude · **Approver:** Zak (§4.5)

Zak: *"Chase w10_n5 properly — look deeply into it… the picks it makes… how it plays out… the
losses… understand what's really happening… learn about the wins… question those appropriately…
and the losses… and learn from them… how could we minimize them and make the Sharpe more stable?"*

§1 is forensics on the stored run and contains no new runs. §2 is the pre-registration, written
before any cell was dispatched. **Every axis in §2 traces to a measured number in §1** — that is the
condition this work order sets itself, because the alternative is a grid of plausible ideas.

The subject is **run 340** (`w10_n5`): five names, one per weekly tranche, `every_sessions=1,
tranches=5, n=5`, top-500 by dollar volume, §3.2's trail, next-open execution. 37.76% CAGR, Sharpe
0.927, −58.6% bootstrap drawdown, 180 names/yr.

---

## 1 · Forensics

### 1.1 The shape of it

| | |
|---|---|
| trades | 1,617 over 9 years, **238 distinct names** |
| win rate | **50.1%** — a coin flip |
| average win / loss | +12.69% / −8.14% (payoff 1.56) |
| average hold | **10 sessions**; winners 12.5, losers 7.5 |
| best / worst trade | +169.7% / −35.4% |
| expectancy | +2.29% per trade |

It wins half the time and wins bigger than it loses. That is a textbook momentum profile and there
is nothing wrong with it.

### 1.2 Where the money comes from — and the exit that loses it

| exit | n | avg P&L | total P&L | avg bars |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| rebalance | 849 | +5.32% | **+$5,129,628** | 10.4 |
| trail_stop | 764 | −1.09% | **−$1,993,254** | 9.6 |
| open_at_end | 4 | +5.77% | +$160,973 | 1.5 |

**Every dollar of return comes from the rebalance. The stop is net negative.** That is not the same
as saying the stop is bad — the counterfactual is unknown until it is run — but it reframes the arm:
this is a *rotation* strategy in which the stop is an overlay, not a stop strategy.

Decomposed by where the stop fired:

| | n | avg | total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| stopped while up >20% | 76 | +46.15% | **+$5,301,142** |
| stopped up 5–20% | 95 | +12.23% | +$2,366,303 |
| stopped up 0–5% | 46 | +2.50% | +$116,535 |
| stopped down 0–5% | 81 | −2.70% | −$158,491 |
| stopped down 5–10% | 205 | −7.80% | −$2,282,757 |
| **stopped down >10%** | **261** | **−14.55%** | **−$7,335,985** |

**The two halves of §3.2 do opposite jobs here.** The *ratchet* — trail 10% below the high once
armed — banks $5.3m on 76 trades. The *initial* stop loses $7.3m on 261.

And note what the bottom row actually says. The initial stop is set at **entry − 8%**. It closes
those 261 trades at an average of **−14.55%**. The difference is gap-through fills: on names of this
volatility the session opens below the stop and the fill is the open, not the stop. **The 8% stop is
not an 8% stop on this book.** That is honest simulation, not a defect — but it means the protection
being paid for is not the protection being received.

### 1.3 The churn, which is the real finding

| exit | re-bought within 21 days | average days out |
|---|---:|---:|
| trail_stop | **599 of 762 — 78.6%** | 6.4 |
| rebalance | 439 of 847 — 51.8% | 5.6 |

**Four out of five stopped names are back in the book inside a fortnight.** The most-traded names:

| name | round trips | window | net P&L |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| NVAX | 29 | 2020-06 → 2021-03 | +$187,695 |
| MCK | 29 | 2022-05 → 2023-03 | **+$7,311** |
| MARA | 29 | 2021-01 → 2021-12 | +$58,342 |
| ACL | 28 | 2018-11 → 2019-09 | **+$294** (3 wins in 28) |
| RIOT | 27 | 2018-01 → 2021-10 | +$30,481 |
| BA | 23 | 2017-08 → 2018-05 | **+$9,042** |
| AXTI | 23 | 2026-03 → 2026-08 | +$18,949 |

**Twenty-eight round trips in ACL for $294.** The same name in the top winners *and* the worst
losers: AXTI appears four times in each. The book is not holding a position; it is sawing one in
half repeatedly, paying spread on every cut.

`docs/yuna_plan.md` §3.2 already legislates the answer and this arm has never implemented it:

> *"A stop-out carries no cooldown — re-entry requires a valid base and all gates, nothing more."*

**A valid base is an observation, not a delay.** The banded arm has one (`base_state`: within 10% of
the 252-day high, above a rising 50-day). A calendar book has none — it re-buys whatever ranks. The
plan is not being followed here, and §10b of the synthesis found the identical defect on the monthly
arm months ago.

### 1.4 Why the Sharpe moves — it is the volatility, and it is not stationary

| year | return | annualized vol | Sharpe | % of exits stopped |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2017 (part) | +6.95% | **19.2%** | 1.022 | 31% |
| 2018 | **−25.28%** | 33.1% | **−0.719** | 37% |
| 2019 | +38.54% | 25.4% | 1.410 | 35% |
| 2020 | +116.26% | 57.0% | 1.634 | 55% |
| 2021 | +33.05% | 58.1% | 0.780 | 63% |
| 2022 | +4.84% | 32.9% | 0.309 | 41% |
| 2023 | +32.22% | 27.8% | 1.154 | 26% |
| 2024 | +99.69% | 42.5% | 1.839 | 43% |
| 2025 | +11.88% | **64.3%** | 0.494 | 64% |
| 2026 (part) | +80.56% | **68.8%** | 1.761 | 70% |

**Realized volatility runs from 19% to 69% and the book does nothing about it.** The annual Sharpe
ranges −0.72 to +1.84. A five-name book of the highest momentum-per-unit-volatility names inherits
whatever volatility the market's speculative end is currently running, unmanaged. That *is* the
Sharpe instability — it is not a return problem, it is an exposure-sizing problem.

The drawdown confirms it. There is no single freak event; there are **five separate ~45% drawdowns**
— 2018-12 (−44.5%), 2020-03 (−43.9%), 2021-05 (−46.3%), 2022-01 (−46.7%, the max), 2025-04 (−44.9%).
**Roughly one every eighteen months.** That is the arm's character, not an accident of one year.

### 1.5 What the forensics did *not* find

**Liquidity is not a problem, and I expected it to be.** A five-name book concentrated in AXTI reads
like an execution risk. Measured, the 60-session average dollar volume at entry:

| MCK | MARA | NVAX | AXTI | OKLO | BA | ACL | RGTI | GME | SMCI |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $373m | $704m | $728m | **$810m** | $1.3bn | $1.4bn | $1.6bn | $1.9bn | $7.7bn | $35bn |

Minimum across every name held: **$272m a day**. The top-500 filter is doing its job. These are
liquid names having volatile years, not thin names.

**Nor is it a three-name result.** The ex-top-3 jackknife beats SPMO. The concentration is in *time*
(2020, 2024 and 2026 carry most of the dollars) rather than in names — and that is partly arithmetic,
since the account is largest in the later years.

## 2 · Pre-registration — six cells, four hypotheses, no guesses

**Committed before dispatch.** The centre is run 340 and is not re-run.

| cell | changes | answers |
|---|---|---|
| `w5_noeuph` | euphoria band 5% → 10% (off) | §1.2 — the ratchet fires on 60%-vol names constantly |
| `w5_door` | §3.2's valid-base gate on entry | §1.3 — 78.6% re-entry inside 21 days |
| `w5_vt40` | volatility target 40% | §1.4 — vol runs 19–69% unmanaged |
| `w5_vt55` | volatility target 55% | §1.4, the looser end |
| `w5_notrail` | no trail; rebalance is the only exit | §1.2 — the stop is net −$2.0m |
| `w5_init15` | initial stop 8% → 15% | §1.2 — an "8%" stop delivering −14.55% |

### Predictions

1. **`w5_door` is the one I expect to work, and it is the only cell that is not a probe.** It
   implements a clause the plan already carries and this arm has been violating. I predict it cuts
   trade count by 25–40% and *raises* Sharpe, with CAGR roughly flat — refusing the broken re-entry
   removes trades that are a coin flip with a gap-risk tail attached.
2. **`w5_vt40` lowers CAGR and raises Sharpe.** That is what a volatility target does; the question
   is the exchange rate. If it gives up more than ~5 points of CAGR for less than ~0.05 of Sharpe it
   is not worth it. **This is the cell aimed squarely at Zak's question.**
3. **`w5_notrail` is the one I am least sure about, and it is the most informative either way.**
   The stop is net −$2.0m, so naively removing it adds $2.0m — but that reasoning is wrong, because
   without the stop those positions run to the next rebalance and the 2020/2022/2025 drawdowns get
   deeper. I predict **CAGR up, Sharpe down, drawdown materially worse**. If Sharpe goes *up* too,
   §3.2's trail is not earning its place on this clock and that is a finding worth a plan
   conversation.
4. **`w5_init15` beats the centre on CAGR.** The 8% initial is inside the noise of a 60%-vol name;
   widening it should convert some of the 261 gap-throughs into positions that survive to the
   rebalance. If it does *not*, the stop is catching real breakdowns rather than noise and the
   churn is the price of genuine protection.

### How stability gets measured

CAGR and Sharpe are not enough for the question asked. Each cell is scored on the **dispersion of
its annual Sharpe** and the **dispersion of its annual realized volatility** across the ten calendar
years in §1.4. The centre's numbers to beat: annual Sharpe ranging −0.72 to +1.84, annual vol
ranging 19.2% to 68.8%. **A cell that raises overall Sharpe while leaving that dispersion untouched
has not answered the question.**

### Bars

- **Stability** — annual-Sharpe standard deviation below the centre's, with CAGR still ≥ 30%.
- **Kill** — any cell below 24.14% CAGR is worse than A6's family mean on return while carrying
  more drawdown and turnover, and is closed out.
- **Not a bar** — the deflated Sharpe. Ruling 3 retired it; these six cells lower it for every arm
  in the ledger, which is the accepted cost.

## 3.1 The finding: the stop is the problem, and the churn is its repair

Read the table as a ladder, because that is what it is. **Every loosening of §3.2's stop improves
both return and risk-adjusted return, monotonically:**

| | initial stop | CAGR | Sharpe |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| centre | 8% + full trail | 37.76% | 0.927 |
| w5_noeuph | 8% + trail, no 5% euphoria band | 39.47% | 0.939 |
| w5_init15 | 15% + trail | 40.30% | 0.945 |
| **w5_notrail** | **none at all** | **43.91%** | **0.954** |

And the mechanism is visible in the trade ledger:

| | trades | avg P&L | **avg hold** | losses worse than −10% | their avg |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| centre | 1,617 | +2.29% | **10.4 bars** | 276 | −14.5% |
| w5_notrail | 1,468 | +6.87% | **23.7 bars** | 205 | −20.1% |

**Removing the stop more than doubles the holding period.** The big losses get deeper (−20.1%
against −14.5%) but there are fewer of them (205 against 276), and in aggregate the damage is
roughly the same. What changes is the winners: they run twice as long, and per-trade P&L triples.

**§3.2's stop on this arm was cutting winners, not protecting against losers.**

That resolves §1.3, which I got wrong. I called the 78.6% re-entry rate "the real finding" and
treated it as a defect. `w5_door` tested that reading directly and refuted it — blocking the
re-entry costs **10.76 points of CAGR** and makes the drawdown 16 points worse. The correct reading:

- **The stop creates the loss.** −$2.0m of it.
- **The re-entry repairs it.** Buying the strong name back is how the book stays in the winner.
- **Removing the stop avoids the loss entirely** — better than repairing it.
- **Blocking the repair while keeping the stop is the worst of both**, which is exactly what
  `w5_door` is and exactly how it scored.

## 3.2 Scored against §2's predictions

**1 · `w5_door` was "the one I expect to work." It is the worst cell in the grid** — −10.76 CAGR,
−0.085 Sharpe, a −63.1% max drawdown, and *more* trades rather than the 25–40% fewer I predicted.
Wrong in direction, magnitude and mechanism.

**2 · Volatility targeting was predicted to lower CAGR and raise Sharpe. It lowered both** at 40%
and was neutral at 55%. It did do its mechanical job — annual vol dispersion fell to 0.152 from
0.178 and the worst year's vol from 68.8% to 60.0% — but the annual *Sharpe* dispersion got worse
and the worst year got worse (−29.8% against −25.3%). **Trailing volatility does not forecast this
book's returns**, so damping exposure by it removes as much good as bad. This is now the second arm
on which Barroso–Santa-Clara has failed, after A6.

**3 · `w5_notrail` — I predicted CAGR up, Sharpe down, and wrote that if Sharpe rose too then
"§3.2's trail is not earning its place on this clock and that is a finding worth a plan
conversation."** CAGR rose 6.15 points, Sharpe rose to 0.954, and costs fell 58%. **That is the
pre-registered trigger, and it has fired.**

**4 · `w5_init15` beats the centre on CAGR — correct**, +2.54 points, and on Sharpe too. Its
drawdown is 12.7 points worse, which I did not predict.

## 3.3 The stability question, answered — and the answer is mostly no

The metric §2 committed to: dispersion of annual Sharpe and annual volatility across the ten years.

| cell | mean annual Sharpe | **sd annual Sharpe** | worst year Sharpe | sd annual vol | vol range | worst year |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| **w5_noeuph** | 0.987 | **0.696** | **−0.476** | 0.190 | 19.2–70.3% | **−19.3%** |
| w10_n5 (centre) | 0.968 | 0.789 | −0.719 | 0.178 | 19.2–68.8% | −25.3% |
| w5_init15 | 0.952 | 0.796 | −0.789 | 0.195 | 20.4–73.4% | −27.0% |
| w5_vt40 | 0.953 | 0.803 | −0.907 | **0.152** | 19.5–60.0% | −29.8% |
| w5_notrail | **0.901** | 0.826 | −0.719 | **0.235** | 19.8–83.9% | −25.1% |
| w5_vt55 | 0.969 | 0.829 | −0.907 | 0.180 | 19.5–65.5% | −29.8% |
| w5_door | 0.985 | **1.417** | −0.822 | 0.130 | 16.9–56.9% | −26.6% |

**Only one cell clears §2's stability bar: `w5_noeuph`.** Annual-Sharpe dispersion 0.696 against the
centre's 0.789, with CAGR at 39.47%. It is also the best cell in the two places stability actually
matters — **worst-year Sharpe −0.476 against −0.719, and worst year −19.3% against −25.3%.**

Everything else was flat or worse. **So the honest answer to "how do we make the Sharpe more
stable" is: with the levers tested, mostly you cannot.** A five-name momentum book has an annual
Sharpe that ranges from −0.7 to +1.8, and that range is what the strategy *is*, not a defect sitting
on top of it waiting to be filtered off. Removing the euphoria tighten buys about 0.09 of dispersion
and a materially better worst year. Nothing else bought anything.

**Note the tension in the two best cells.** `w5_notrail` has the highest *overall* Sharpe (0.954)
and the **lowest mean annual** Sharpe (0.901) with the **widest** vol range (up to 83.9%). Its
overall number benefits from compounding through the big years; its year-to-year experience is the
least stable in the grid. `w5_noeuph` is the reverse. **They answer different questions**, and which
one is right depends on whether Zak is optimising the ten-year number or the experience of living
through it.

## 3.4 The caveat that outweighs everything above

**`w5_notrail` removes §3.2's stop entirely, and this window contains no momentum crash.**

Every drawdown figure here — including that −60.2% bootstrap — is resampled from 2017–2026. There is
no 2008 and no 2001 in it. **A no-stop, five-name, 100%-invested momentum book is precisely the
configuration that a momentum crash destroys**, and the data cannot see one. The measured cost of
removing the stop is 1.6 points of bootstrap drawdown; the unmeasured cost is the left tail this
window never sampled.

This is not a reason to reject `w5_notrail`. It is a reason that **its 43.91% must not be compared
against the centre's 37.76% as though the two carry the same tail risk.** They do not, and no number
in this document can tell you by how much. That gap is what the 2003–2016 backfill buys, and it is
the third time this programme has arrived at the same door.

# 4 · Adversarial audit of `w5_notrail`

Zak: *"Check the numbers for real. Double check every digit… Really TRY to find a problem with it…
Why might it be inflated."* Six checks. Five cleared. The sixth is the answer, and it is not a bug.

## 4.1 What survived

**Arithmetic.** $200,000 → $5,273,482 over 2,260 sessions. Recomputed CAGR 44.03% against 43.91%
stored — a day-count convention, not an error.

**Stale marks — the one I most expected to find.** No stop plus 23.7-bar holds means a name that
goes dark is carried at its last print and sold at it. Measured: **17 of 1,468 exit legs priced more
than three days stale, none beyond ten days, $30,371 of P&L against $5.07m.** 0.6%. Not it.

**The 58% cost drop**, which read as too good when I first wrote it up. Gross dollars traded fell
from **$213.2m to $136.4m** — removing the stop removes the round trips, and costs follow volume.
Explicable, not suspicious.

**Name concentration.** Trade-level jackknife: all 26.08×, ex-top-3 **14.39×**, ex-top-5 11.11×,
against SPMO's 4.56×. Removing the five best trades still triples the benchmark. Skew is *positive*
(+0.37). Not a lottery ticket.

**Liquidity.** Established in §1.5 — minimum 60-session ADDV across every name held is $272m.

**Execution lag — and this refuted my own hypothesis.** §1 found an asymmetry that looked damning:
`rank_at(i)` reads bar i and the book trades at `adj[i]`, the same close, while 47% of the
*centre's* exits are stops filled at the next open. Removing the stop removes the only
conservatively-filled exits in the model, so the margin could have been execution rather than
strategy. The falsifier had never been run on the calendar path at all. Built, and run:

| | lag 0 | lag 1 | lag 2 | gap vs centre |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `w5_notrail` | 43.91% | 43.08% | 46.59% | — |
| centre `w10_n5` | 37.76% | 37.01% | — | — |
| **advantage** | **+6.15** | **+6.07** | — | **holds** |

**Both arms lose the same ~0.8 points to a one-session lag, and the advantage survives intact.**
The one-bar edge is real and worth about 0.8 points to every calendar arm in the ledger — worth
knowing, and applied evenly. My suspicion was wrong.

## 4.2 What did not survive — the advantage is two years

| year | centre | `w5_notrail` | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2017 (part) | +7.0% | 0.0% | −7.0 |
| 2018 | −25.3% | −25.1% | +0.2 |
| 2019 | +38.5% | +42.7% | +4.1 |
| **2020** | +116.3% | **+169.0%** | **+52.8** |
| 2021 | +33.1% | +30.8% | −2.2 |
| **2022** | +4.8% | **−1.5%** | **−6.3** |
| 2023 | +32.2% | +44.1% | +11.9 |
| **2024** | +99.7% | **+62.1%** | **−37.6** |
| **2025** | +11.9% | **+71.3%** | **+59.5** |
| 2026 (part) | +80.6% | +78.0% | −2.6 |

**The entire +6.15-point advantage is 2020 and 2025.** In the other eight years the no-stop book is
*behind* in five of them, including losing by **37.6 points in 2024**. Take those two years out and
removing the stop is not an improvement — it is a mild deterioration.

**And the two years share a shape.** 2020 is the COVID crash and its V-shaped recovery; 2025 is the
April tariff crash and its V-shaped recovery. The mechanism is plain: **in a crash that snaps back,
the stop sells the bottom and the no-stop book holds through it.** That is a real effect and it is
worth real money — when the crash snaps back.

**Now read 2022, the only grinding bear market in the window. `w5_notrail` loses 6.3 points there.**
That is the tell, and it is the whole argument: when the decline is slow and does not recover
quickly, the stop earns its place, exactly as §3.2 intends.

## 4.3 The verdict

**The 43.91% is not inflated by a defect. It is conditional on crash shape, and this window samples
only one shape.**

2017–2026 contains four drawdowns and three of them are V-shaped (2018, 2020, 2025). The one that
ground (2022) is the one the no-stop book lost. A momentum crash — 2008, 2001 — is a grind, not a
V, and the window contains neither.

So the honest statement is narrower than "removing the stop is worth 6 points":

> **On a window whose crashes all snapped back, removing the stop was worth 6 points, and the two
> years that produced it were both snap-backs. In the one grinding decline available, it cost 6.3
> points.**

Three further caveats stand, none of which the audit resolved:

- **End-point sensitivity.** CAGR ranges **24.7% to 51.1%** across end dates in the last four years
  (the centre: 21.8% to 41.6%). 100% of terminal NAV is unrealized, $881k of it paper gain. The
  *relative* advantage held at every endpoint (+3.4 to +8.6), but 43.91% is not a stable number and
  must never be quoted as one.
- **`unproven`.** DSR 0.793 against a 0.95 bar at 200+ trials; bootstrap 5th-percentile CAGR 10.72%,
  below SPMO's 21.38%.
- **Kurtosis 8.15** with a −60.2% bootstrap drawdown and five ~45% drawdowns in nine years.

**Recommendation: do not adopt `w5_notrail`.** Not because a number is wrong — I could not find a
wrong number — but because the thing it is measured on is not in the data. §3.2's stop is insurance
against a grinding decline, this window contains one grinding decline, and the stop won it. Removing
the stop is a bet that the next crash is a V. **The backfill is what prices that bet**, and this is
now the third time the programme has arrived at that door — which is itself the finding worth acting
on.

### A note on authority

`w5_notrail`, `w5_init15` and `w5_noeuph` vary §3.2's own stop, which is the plan's law. They are
**probes, exactly as WO-A5's ladder was** — run to price the plan's choices, not to replace them.
Adopting any of them into the sleeve requires a plan amendment and Zak's ruling. `w5_door` is the
opposite case: it moves the arm *toward* the plan rather than away from it.
