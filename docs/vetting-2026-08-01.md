# Vetting the compounder scores and hurdles

`verify` run 1, against production, after the conformance re-score (`score` run 49). Zak's question
was whether the scores and hurdles are working, and if not what to change in the plan. The answer is
that the pipeline runs correctly and **two of its rules combine into a result the plan did not
intend**. Neither finding is a coding error in the usual sense; both are the specified behaviour,
faithfully implemented, producing a bench that ranks the wrong thing.

---

Fifteen for fifteen. Not one full-confidence name appears until below CCN 84, and the three
businesses I recommended in R5 — DOCS 72.0, TTD 71.6, ADSK 61.1, all with real, cross-checked
engines — rank **below every single name above**.

### Why, mechanically

§3.3 says drop a missing component and renormalize the rest to 100. The remaining two components are
cash conversion and size. Names whose engine cannot be measured are overwhelmingly names with
unusual cash-flow shapes — which is to say, names that score *high* on cash conversion — and the
funnel's size component rewards smallness hardest. So:

**Dropping the engine raises the score.** FVRR scores 97.4 on `(96.5 + 98.3) / 2`. Had its engine
been computable at, say, the 30th percentile, its CCN would be `(30 + 96.5 + 98.3) / 3 = 74.9` and it
would rank below DOCS. The data-confidence path pays a company for having unmeasurable fundamentals.

That is the incentive exactly inverted, and it is worth being blunt about the cause: **my Phase H
conformance fix made it worse.** Tightening the cross-check to a flat 5pp (correct, per §3.1) and
routing failures down the data-confidence path (correct, per §3.3) increased the number of dropped
engines — and every dropped engine is a promotion.

### The second mechanism behind it

§3.1 floors reinvestment at zero. For an asset-light business whose working capital *releases* cash,
`(capex − D&A + ΔWC)` is negative, reinvestment floors to 0, and the engine reads 0 for a company
compounding at 20%+. This was recorded in `learnings.md` #16 against ANET, AVGO and VRT. It is the
same defect seen from the other side: the engine is unmeasurable for a large, systematic class of
exactly the businesses this sleeve exists to own.

## Finding 2 — stored hurdles do not reproduce the 15% floor, and the error runs both ways

`verify` rebuilds each stored hurdle from the row's own components. Eleven mismatches in the tail
alone:

| Ticker | Stored hurdle | ER at that price | Price that actually clears 15% | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TTD | 75.05 | 11.8% | 64.42 | overstated +17% |
| PINS | 23.59 | 13.4% | 19.94 | overstated +18% |
| NBIX | 65.88 | 13.7% | 59.24 | overstated +11% |
| PN | 2.04 | 13.5% | 1.84 | overstated +11% |
| TNET | 50.74 | 14.1% | 47.57 | overstated |
| IDT | 15.84 | 14.1% | 14.86 | overstated |
| NVR | 3064.06 | 14.4% | 2951.36 | overstated |
| NXT | 40.66 | 14.3% | 37.25 | overstated |
| NEXN | 5.76 | 14.3% | 5.65 | overstated |
| **RDWR** | 5.32 | **16.8%** | 5.96 | **understated** |
| **GFS** | 13.05 | **15.9%** | 13.86 | **understated** |

**The two-way direction is the diagnosis.** A one-sided formula error cannot produce both. What can
is a *timing* inconsistency: the hurdle's share count is derived from a price, and the price it was
derived from is no longer the price stored beside it.

§3.1 (ruling B5) says cap-at-P uses **effective shares = vendor cap ÷ last close**. That resolves ADR
ratios and share classes elegantly — and it makes the hurdle a function of today's quote. The
scheduled 02:00 UTC nightly ran after `score` did, ingested new bars, and `duties.refresh_marks`
rewrote `bench.last_close` while leaving `hurdle_price` alone. Names that rose now show understated
hurdles; names that fell show overstated ones. Exactly the pattern above.

**Nothing in the plan is violated.** The hurdle is simply not price-invariant, which it needs to be
to mean what §3.1 says it means: a price at which the *business* clears 15%.

## What I recommend changing in the plan

Three edits, in order of value. Announced properly: exact section, current text, proposed text.

### Edit 1 — §3.1 CCN: an unmeasurable engine makes a name bench-ineligible

> **Current** (§3.1 CCN table, Compounding engine row): "Requires ≥ 3 fiscal years, else →
> data-confidence path (§3.3)"
>
> **Proposed**: "Requires ≥ 3 fiscal years and a passing cross-check. A name whose compounding
> engine cannot be computed, or whose engine fails the §3.1 reliability check, is **not eligible for
> the bench** — the data-confidence path applies to cash conversion and size, never to the engine."

Rationale: the component is named "the compounding engine" and weighted a third. A compounder ranked
without one is a category error, and under renormalization it is actively rewarded. Consequence,
accepted: the bench shrinks a great deal, and what remains is full-confidence names.

### Edit 2 — §3.1 formulas: fall back to observed growth rather than dropping the engine

> **Current**: "Reinvestment rate = (capex − D&A + ΔWC) ÷ NOPAT, floored at 0, capped at 150%."
>
> **Proposed**: add — "Where the cash-flow-derived engine is unmeasurable or fails the cross-check,
> the engine is **observed 3-year revenue growth**, capped at 25% and marked `growth-derived`.
> Growth = ROIC × reinvestment is an identity, so the observed side of that identity is the honest
> substitute — and it is the same number the cross-check already treats as ground truth."

This is the edit I would most want. It fixes both halves of Finding 1 at once: asset-light
compounders stop reading as engine 0, and dropping the engine stops being a promotion, because there
is no longer anything to drop. It also makes Edit 1 far less destructive — most names become
scoreable on a number we can verify instead of being excluded.

### Edit 3 — §3.1 hurdle: freeze the share count with the filing

> **Current**: "cap at price P uses **effective shares = vendor cap ÷ last close**"
>
> **Proposed**: "cap at price P uses **effective shares = vendor cap ÷ the close on the date that cap
> was reported**, stored with the filing. The hurdle changes when a filing changes FCF, growth or the
> fair multiple — never because the quote moved. `gap_to_hurdle` carries the price movement."

Without this the hurdle silently decays every night. This one is closest to a plain bug and I would
implement it the moment you approve the wording.

## Consequences for the R5 package already with you

**Hold the three approvals.** DOCS and TTD are both on the mismatch list, and their real hurdles are
lower than the ones the memos quote — TTD's by 17%. The businesses are unchanged and the memos stand
on their merits, but "at or below hurdle" is not currently a number I can defend for either, and
approving them would arm entries against it. ADSK reconciles exactly and is unaffected.

The right sequence is: rule on the edits above → re-run `score` → re-run `verify` until it is clean →
then approve against numbers that reconcile. That is one working session, not a rebuild.

## What is working, for the avoidance of doubt

Four of six hand-checked names reconciled to within 0.2%, and the great majority of the bench is not
on the mismatch list. C1, the percentile machinery, the cash-conversion component, the drag term and
the solver are all doing exactly what §3.1 specifies — the solver is exact to the cent when handed
consistent inputs. Both findings are about *which inputs reach it* and *what the renormalization
rewards*, which is why both fixes are plan edits rather than patches.
