## One decision blocks everything

**A2 needs a slot-ordering rule, and it is Zak's to make.**

The book holds 30. On most days in a bull market, more than 30 names make a new 252-session high.
Something has to decide who gets the slots, and today that something is MCN — inherited from §3.2,
never specified by E3. MCN rewards explosive short-horizon momentum, so a mega-cap grinding out
+30%/yr never outranks a small-cap that ran 40% in six weeks.

The consequence is measured, not theoretical: across runs 54, 55 and 56, **MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, AVGO,
LLY and NFLX were never bought once in nine years**, and NVDA was held twice for a loss. All are
`in_l0`, active, 2,244 bars, eligible every single day.

Defensible orderings, each selecting a completely different book:

- first come, first served by breakout date — truest to "buy the breakout when it happens"
- longest continuous time above the 200-day
- furthest above the 200-day
- lowest volatility of the trend
- market cap

E3 names none of them. Picking one silently would be inventing the strategy rather than testing it.

## Standing rule to adopt

**No arm's number is believed until a capture audit runs.** Runs 54, 55 and 56 all passed their
accounting invariants, produced plausible CAGRs, and were each measuring something other than the
specified arm. Four defects — a breakeven stop hidden in the confirmation machine, an entry door
that was never gated, A1's candidate pool, A1's candidate order — and **not one was visible in a
summary statistic.** The question that found all of them was "did it buy NVIDIA".

The audit has three parts, and only the first exists so far (as ad-hoc SQL, in arms-2026-08-12.md):

1. **The net** — enumerate every large momentum push in the window mechanically (name-months
   running +50%+ off a 252-high). What fraction did the arm enter at all?
2. **The ride** — for each one caught, what share of the move was kept? Entered near the start and
   held, or clipped after three days?
3. **The junk** — of everything held, what share was neither a push nor caught one?

Build it as a reusable script so every arm gets it automatically. CAGR alone cannot distinguish
"caught 70% of the pushes and kept 40% of each" from "caught 10% and got lucky twice".
