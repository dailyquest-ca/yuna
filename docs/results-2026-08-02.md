# Yuna — state of the system, 2 Aug 2026

A self-contained account of what the machine currently does, what it produced on today's
full production run, and the four problems I can prove are real. Written to be read
alongside `docs/yuna_plan.md` (the law) by someone who has not seen this repo.

---

## 1. What the system is trying to do

Find ~10 excellent US-listed businesses, hold 4–6 at a time, aim at ~30%/yr, buy only at
sane prices, and drop losers fast. Two sleeves: **compounders** (quality, bought below a
computed entry price) and **momentum** (separate rules, not covered here).

The compounder funnel, in order:

1. **L0** — the tradable universe (~2,760 US names after listing/liquidity filters).
2. **Gate C1** — hard disqualifiers: FCF not positive, net issuance too high, net
   debt/EBITDA too high, net debt growing faster than EBITDA, deposit-takers excluded,
   too little history. **634 of 2,534 scored names pass.**
3. **CCN score** — three equal-weight percentile ranks *within L0*:
   - **engine** — the compounding rate
   - **cash conversion** — how much of profit becomes cash
   - **durability** — consistency/quality of the record
   Top 60 by CCN become the **bench**. (Currently 64 rows carry a rank; the extra few are
   holdings scored outside the top 60.)
4. **Hurdle price** — the highest price we may pay. Everything below turns on this.
5. Sessions R1–R5 (scheduled Claude runs) read the database and write briefs/tickets.
   No session ever computes a score by hand.

## 2. The valuation math, exactly as it now stands

Definitions (glossary, as amended today):

```
FCF          = CFO − capex − SBC            (stock comp is a real cost; applied everywhere)
fair         = min(the stock's own 5-yr median P/FCF, 30×)      [flat 25× if < 8 priced quarters]
FCF yield    = FCF_ttm / market cap at price P
drag         = derating drag toward `fair`
ER(P)        = FCF yield(P) + growth − drag
hurdle       = the highest P where ER(P) ≥ 0.15
```

Two rules were added today by announced edit and approved:

**(a) Growth is capped at `0.15 − 1/fair`.**
Rationale via the Gordon identity `h = 1/M + g` (h = hurdle return, M = multiple paid):
if you pay multiple M and grow at g, your return is roughly `1/M + g`. Requiring
`h ≥ 0.15` while paying no more than `fair` implies `g ≤ 0.15 − 1/fair`. Growth above
that lets the solver justify a price above `fair`, which the second rule forbids.

**(b) The hurdle is hard-capped at `fair × FCF/share`** — never pay a richer multiple
than the stock's own history. Universal, no exceptions.

Both are implemented in one solver (`src/signals.py: hurdle_price`), used by scoring,
verification and backtests alike.

### ⚠️ The consequence nobody registered in advance

**On 53 of 63 ranked names (84%), the growth cap binds.** When it binds, `g = 0.15 − 1/fair`
exactly, so `h = 1/fair + (0.15 − 1/fair) = 0.15` identically — the ER solve lands on
`fair × FCF/share`, which is also where cap (b) puts it. The two rules coincide.

**Therefore, for 84% of the bench, the entire valuation apparatus reduces to:**

```
hurdle = fair multiple × FCF per share
```

The growth estimate, the FCF-yield term, the derating drag and the 15% floor contribute
nothing to the answer. They are arithmetically inert. You can verify this on any row:

| ticker | fair | g used | 0.15 − 1/fair | binds? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MELI | 16.3 | 0.089 | 0.0887 | yes |
| HRMY | 10.1 | 0.051 | 0.0510 | yes |
| AGX  | 12.4 | 0.069 | 0.0694 | yes |
| GCT  |  6.0 | 0.000 | −0.017 → 0 | yes (floored at 0) |
| BKNG | 30.0 | 0.117 | 0.1167 | yes |
| SPGI | 28.8 | 0.111 | 0.1153 | **no** (real g is lower) |

The 10 names where it does *not* bind are the only ones whose growth estimate still moves
the price: CNQ, FIGS, FTDR, MKTX, PIPR, QLYS, RHLD, SCHW, SPGI, VRRM.

This may be defensible as policy — "never pay above the stock's own median multiple" is a
real discipline, and it is exactly ruling (b) as approved. But **the plan still describes
the entry rule as a 15% expected-return hurdle, and that description is now false for 84%
of names.** Under the plan's own "plain-language law" (every rule must be explainable in one
honest sentence), this needs either a rewrite of §3.1's description or a change to the
mechanism. **This is the single most important open question.**

## 4. The four problems I can prove

### Problem 1 — the entry math has collapsed to one rule (§2 above)
84% of the bench prices off `fair × FCF/share` alone. Not necessarily wrong; definitely not
what the plan says. Needs a decision.

### Problem 2 — 61 of 64 bench names have no *measured* engine
`engine_provenance` is `growth-derived` on 61 rows and `measured` on 3. The plan's waterfall
says: try to measure reinvestment economics; if a 5-percentage-point cross-check fails, fall
back to deriving the engine from growth, capped at 25%. **The fallback has become the rule.**
The measurement path is failing almost universally — the underlying cause is how reinvestment
is measured, which is a known TODO, not yet designed. Consequence: the "engine" third of the
CCN score is a growth-rate proxy for nearly every name, so CCN is closer to
"growth + cash conversion + durability" than the plan claims.

### Problem 3 — the bench is full of cyclicals at peak, not compounders
Largest industry blocks on today's bench:

- **Engineering & Construction — 7 names**: AGX, FIX, STRL, PRIM, PWR, EME, MTZ
- Software-Infrastructure — 7: DLO, GCT, PAY, CPAY, FTNT, EEFT, QLYS
- Software-Application — 6: NOW, SPSC, INTU, PCTY, MANH, RDVT
- Capital Markets — 5 · Credit Services — 3 · Semiconductors — 3 · Asset Management — 3

Seven E&C contractors plus two oilfield-services names is a data-centre construction boom
being read as durable compounding. These businesses have real cash flows *right now* and
weak records across a full cycle — exactly what a trailing-3-year screen with a growth-derived
engine will over-reward.

### Problem 4 — the great investors own almost nothing we own
The new "company we keep" check compares our bench against holdings of seven reference
investors (Fundsmith, Akre, Polen, TCI, Pershing, WCM, Giverny), read from stored filings.

**Only 6 bench names are held by any of them**: DLO (Polen), NOW (Polen), FTNT (Fundsmith),
SPGI (TCI), TSM (WCM), CAH (WCM, unranked). That is 5 of 64 ranked names — under 8%.

Meanwhile, names they hold that pass our own C1 gate but never reach the bench include
**ADP, CPRT, IDXX, CHD, ASML, MCO, CSGP, GDDY, CTVA, GE, APP, ILMN, IBP**. These are
excluded by CCN rank, not by any quality gate. That is the classic-compounder list, and our
screen ranks E&C contractors above all of it.

Two names they hold fail C1 on debt rules: CP (net debt/EBITDA 2.7×) and ZTS (net debt
growing faster than EBITDA). Those are judgement calls our gate makes more strictly than
they do.

> not bench names (6). The counter has been fixed to report both figures separately. The

## 6. Data-quality caveats that affect every number above

- **SBC coverage is partial.** 51 of 64 bench rows carry the `sbc_missing` stamp, meaning at
  least one period in the history had no vendor SBC figure and fell back to reported FCF.
  61 rows do have a TTM SBC figure. So the TTM is mostly SBC-net while the 3-year history
  behind the engine and cash-conversion scores is only partly so — an internal inconsistency
  that flatters historical growth relative to the present.
- **Vendor cap and shares.** Effective share count is frozen at filing time (vendor market
  cap ÷ close on the cap's as-of date), so the hurdle does not move with the quote. Corporate
  actions between filings are handled by exception.
- **Fair multiple uses a 3-year window, not the plan's 5.** Under 8 priced quarters a name
  gets a flat 25×. This is an announced, unratified deviation (score.py docstring).
- **CNQ.TO carries a negative growth figure (−0.078)** and still sits on the bench as a
  holding scored outside L0.

## 7. What I would ask another reviewer to rule on

1. **The collapsed hurdle.** Given 84% of names price at `fair × FCF/share`, should the plan
   drop the expected-return framing and state the multiple rule plainly — or should the
   growth cap be loosened so the ER machinery does real work? (My view: the rule is sound,
   the description is false, and describing it honestly is the smaller change. But a system
   whose entry price ignores growth entirely will pay the same multiple for a 4% grower and a
   20% grower, and that is a genuine analytical loss.)
2. **The engine.** With 61/64 growth-derived, is CCN measuring what it claims? What is the
   right way to measure reinvestment economics from vendor statements?
3. **The cyclicals.** Seven E&C contractors in a 64-name quality bench. Is the fix a
   cycle-aware durability term, an industry exclusion, a longer history window, or something
   else?
4. **The corroboration gap.** Our screen and seven great investors agree on 5 names out of 64.
   Either the screen is finding overlooked value, or it is systematically selecting against
   quality. Which, and how would you test it?
