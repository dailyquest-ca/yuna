# WO-A9 — The deep test: does the no-stop book survive a momentum crash?

**Issued:** 2026-08-14 · **Author:** Claude · **Approver:** Zak (§4.5)

**§3's predictions were committed before any deep cell ran**, while the backfill was still

## 1 · The question

WO-A8 §4.1 found that `w5_notrail`'s entire +6.15-point advantage over the stopped book comes from
**two years, 2020 and 2025, and both are V-shaped crash-and-recover.** In 2022 — the one grinding
bear market in the ten-year window — the no-stop book *lost* 6.3 points.

> **On a window whose crashes all snapped back, removing the stop was worth 6 points. Removing the
> stop is a bet that the next crash is a V.**

**A momentum crash is not a V.** The canonical one — March to June 2009 — is a grinding reversal in
which momentum lost roughly three quarters of its value as the most beaten-down junk rallied
hardest. It is the event Barroso–Santa-Clara is about, and it is the single event most dangerous to
a concentrated, fully-invested, unstopped momentum book.

The ten-year window contains no such event. This work order buys one.

## 2 · What the backfill actually did

Three passes, all through the repo's own `src/backfill.py`, which fetches each candidate, screens it
in memory against L0's own floor — 210 sessions, $5 price, $10m 50-session median dollar volume at
the best stretch it ever had — and **stores only the qualifiers.**

| pass | run | result |
|---|---|---|
| live universe → 2005 (`what=bars, years=21`) | 386 | 2,816 targets, **5,914,109 bars**, stopped on WAL pressure, resumable |
| benchmark candidates (`PDP.US, RSP.US`) | 387 | **10,173 bars — complete** (PDP 4,895 + RSP 5,278) |
| delisted census → 2005 (`what=delisted, years=21`) | 388 | in flight at time of writing |

**The WAL guard fired twice and that is the guard working**, not a failure — it exists because the
2026-08-03 outage was a WAL burst that killed the database at under 1 GB. Both runs committed what
they had and reported `amber` with a resume instruction.

### Why the delisted pass is the one that matters

Before it, the store held **2,038 delisted names of which exactly one died before 2016.** That is
not a census of the dead — it is the 2016–2026 death cohort. The previous delisted run used a
ten-year window, so a company that died in 2009 returned zero bars inside it, failed the minimum-bar
screen, and was dropped.

**A 2008 test run on that universe would have held only companies that survived to 2016**, which
would flatter the crash beyond usefulness — the exact failure mode that makes a backfill worse than
no backfill. Verified before dispatch that the vendor genuinely has the era's dead: **LEH.US** priced
through 2008-09-15 (close $0.21 on 469m shares) and **CCTYQ.US**, Circuit City's bankruptcy ticker.

## 3 · Two defects found while wiring the test

**SPMO does not exist before 2015-10-12.** The strategy parks idle cash in SPMO and is benchmarked
against it. Neither is available for 2008. Zak was asleep and had not ruled on a substitute, and
choosing one silently would be choosing the answer — parking in SPY measures the book against the
market, parking in a momentum ETF measures it against its own factor, and 2008–09 separated those
two violently. **So the ruling becomes an axis:** the deep test runs against every candidate park
and reports each.

| park candidate | first bar | what it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **PDP.US** | 2007-03-01 | Invesco DWA Momentum — *the* like-for-like: did the book beat a momentum ETF through the crash? |
| SPY.US | 2003-08-18 | the market baseline |
| RSP.US | ≤2005-01-03 | equal-weight S&P 500 |

**And a silent one: the market calendar is taken from the benchmark's own bars, and the benchmark
was VOO — first stored bar 2016-08-12.** A 2006 window left alone would have produced an empty
grid and a run that completed reporting nothing. That is the same class of defect that invalidated
runs 95–164. The calendar is now switchable (SPY reaches 2003-08-18) and an empty window raises with
both the requested range and the calendar's real span.

The **window is now part of the `param_hash`**, so a deep-window run cannot collide with a ten-year
run of the same cell — otherwise the deflation would treat "does this survive 2008" and "which
parameter is best on 2017–2026" as the same trial.

## 4 · The cells

Window **2006-01-01 → 2013-12-31**, calendar SPY, ~253 sessions of formation so the book trades from
roughly 2007-01. Three cells against three parks.

| cell | what it is |
|---|---|
| `w5_notrail` | the selected arm — five names, weekly staggered, **no stop** |
| `w10_n5` | the same book **with §3.2's stop** — the control, and the whole comparison |
| `w5_noeuph` | the stop minus the euphoria tighten — WO-A8's only stability winner |

## 5 · Predictions, committed with no result in hand

1. **`w5_notrail` loses to `w10_n5` over this window, reversing the ten-year result.** This is the
   central prediction and it follows directly from §4.1's mechanism. If the no-stop book wins here
   too, the crash-shape theory is wrong and the arm is much stronger than WO-A8 concluded.
2. **Both arms take a drawdown deeper than the −60% bootstrap estimate** — probably −70% or worse
   for the unstopped one. A five-name fully-invested momentum book through 2008-09 has no defence.
3. **The damage concentrates in March–June 2009, not in the 2008 crash itself.** 2008 was a decline
   the whole market shared; the momentum crash is the *reversal*, and it is the reversal that kills
   a book selecting on twelve-month winners.
4. **The park choice moves the benchmark far more than it moves the strategy.** At `sleeve=1.00` the
   book is nearly always invested, so the park barely touches its return — but PDP fell much harder
   than SPY in 2009, so *what the book is measured against* changes the verdict substantially. I
   expect the arm to look best against PDP and worst against SPY.
5. **Deflated Sharpe improves for every arm** simply because a longer, harder window is more
   evidence — this is the one axis on which more data helps rather than hurts.

## 6 · What would change the decision

- **The no-stop book survives 2009 within a few points of the stopped one** → the §4.3 bet is
  substantially de-risked and `w5_notrail` stands as selected.
- **The no-stop book is destroyed and the stopped one is not** → §3.2's stop earns its place on
  evidence, and the ten-year +6 points is confirmed as a window artifact. That is a live-money
  decision made for the cost of one night's compute.
- **Both are destroyed** → the honest reading is that a five-name concentrated momentum book is not
  survivable through a momentum crash at any stop setting, and the sizing question in §1 of the
  synthesis becomes the only question that matters.

**This is a specialized test, not a regime change.** Routine work stays on the ten-year window; the
deep cells are dispatch-only, carry their own window in the params, and are labelled as such.

# Results

## 7 · The measurement

**Runs 355, 356, 357.** Window 2006-01-03 → 2013-12-31, park and calendar SPY, $200k. The book
trades from roughly 2007-01 after its 253-session formation, so the scored equity is **1,760
sessions ≈ 6.98 years**.

| | total | CAGR | max DD | bootstrap DD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **SPY**, the same 1,760 sessions | **+52.2%** | **6.20%** | — | — |
| `w5_notrail` — the selected arm | +35.0% | 4.39% | **−72.8%** | — |
| `w5_noeuph` — WO-A8's stability winner | +7.8% | 1.08% | −68.6% | −59.6% |
| `w10_n5` — the same book with §3.2's stop | **−12.8%** | **−1.94%** | −71.1% | −61.6% |

**The selected arm returned 4.39% a year and lost 72.8% at its worst. SPY returned 6.20% for no
decisions and no spreads.** The stopped version lost money outright over seven years, and
`w5_noeuph` — the one change WO-A8 found improved Sharpe stability — was *worse* here than doing
nothing.

Two wider benchmarks over the full 2006–2013 span, for context: **RSP** (equal-weight S&P 500)
+90.1% / 8.38%, and **PDP** — the momentum ETF — +56.8% / 6.80% from its 2007-03 inception. **The
concentrated five-name book did not beat the factor it is built from.**

For contrast, the same two cells on 2017–2026 returned **43.91%** and **37.76%**.

**A correction to an earlier reading.** The first numbers I reported from this window — 3.87% and
−2.89% — came from a dry run, which prints CAGR over the whole 2,013-session grid including the
253 formation sessions the book does not trade. The persisted runs score the post-warmup equity,
which is the honest denominator, and give 4.39% and −1.94% against a benchmark of 6.20% rather
than 7.03%. **The gap narrows slightly; the verdict does not change.**

**The jackknife is uninformative here and should not be quoted.** `ex_top_3` reads 0.000 for both
scored cells, which is the statistic flooring on a book whose total return is at or below zero —
there is no winner-concentration story to tell about a strategy that did not make money.

## 9 · The caveat that governs everything above

**The delisted census did not complete, so this ran on a survivor-only universe.**

The census is 32,611 US delisted common stocks. The probe screen built for it works — it cut the
fetch list correctly and real 2008-era casualties landed (AirTran, AboveNet, Abitibibowater,
Applied Biosystems). But **every attempt stops after about four minutes on WAL pressure**: pg_wal
was saturated by pass 1's 5.9M bars and does not checkpoint down between attempts. It reached
**A–AC only**. Lehman, Bear Stearns, Merrill, Countrywide, Fannie, Freddie, Ambac and National City
were all verified present at the vendor and **none of them were fetched.**

So the 2008 ranking universe here is ~951 names, **every one of which survived to 2016.**

**The direction of that bias is not ambiguous.** Survivorship removes the names that went to zero.
A momentum book buys names printing new highs, and a fraction of those are the ones that then
collapse — so the missing names are disproportionately names this strategy would have *held*, and
held *down*. **It flatters the strategy, and it flatters the unstopped arm most**, because an
unstopped book holds a collapsing name all the way to the bottom while a stopped one exits.

Which means:

- **"This does not survive 2006–2013" is safe.** Adding the dead can only make it worse.
- **"It returned 3.87%" is not a number to quote.** The real figure is lower by an unknown amount.
- **"Removing the stop won here" is the least safe claim in this document**, because it is precisely
  the claim the missing data would most attack.

## 10 · Verdict

**The ten-year result does not generalise.** Against SPY over 2006–2013 the selected arm earned
about half the return for twenty more points of drawdown, and it did not beat a momentum ETF. That
is measured on a universe biased in the arm's favour.

WO-A8 §4.3 said removing the stop was a bet that the next crash is a V. The deep test does not
settle that bet — it produces something more basic and more damaging: **on the one window in this
programme that contains a real crash, the strategy itself, stop or no stop, was worse than buying
the index.**

**What would overturn this:** finishing the delisted census. Nothing else. The finding cannot get
better with more data — the bias runs one way — but the *magnitude* is unmeasured and the arm
deserves the complete test before it is closed out on a partial one.

**Operationally the backfill is two thirds built and the remainder is a scheduling problem, not a
research one.** The live universe reaches 2005, the benchmarks are in, the census algorithm is
correct and tested. What it needs is for the write-ahead log to drain between passes — a few idle
hours, or a larger instance for an afternoon.
