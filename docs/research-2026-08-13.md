# Research 2026-08-13 — after run 58: what the evidence says the next arm looks like

Zak's question, verbatim in spirit: *a strong momentum play has the highest shot of outsized
returns — what combination of the information we have, and of what the proven practitioners
teach, hones the strategy?* This memo answers with sources. It proposes; nothing in it becomes
law without a ruled work order, and anything not Yuna-specific belongs upstream in
`momentum-doctrine` first.

## 1 · Calibration first: what "outsized" verifiably means

- Academic cross-sectional momentum, the oldest and most replicated anomaly in the literature:
  **~8–9%/yr over 150 years** of data (long–short, gross).
- The best live systematic implementation available to us: **SPMO, +19.79%/yr over the last ten
  years against the S&P's 13.07%** (+457% vs +262%), capturing ~113% of index upside and only
  ~85% of its downside — for a 0.13% fee.
- The famous outliers are real but are a different sport: Minervini's audited **155% (1997) and
  334.8% (2021)** championship years, David Ryan's three straight triple-digit years — all
  single-year, small-account, extremely concentrated, discretionary sprints. No verified
  *systematic* record compounds anywhere near 50%/yr across a decade.

So the honest north star for a systematic sleeve is **high-teens to low-twenties CAGR with a
shallower drawdown than the index** — which is exactly SPMO's profile, which is why E4 is the
bar every in-house idea must clear. 50%/yr is the sprint-contest regime, not a compounding plan.

## 3 · The external evidence, bucket by bucket

### The junk → rank by trend quality, not by event

- **Clenow, *Stocks on the Move***: rank the universe by the **90-day exponential regression
  slope (annualized) × R²** — how fast, times how *cleanly*. Disqualify anything under its own
  100-day MA or with a recent >15% gap; enter only when the index is above its 200-day MA;
  size by ATR risk parity; **exit when the name decays out of the ranking or below its MA — no
  price-noise stop at all.** Independent replications land around 8.8%/yr with **−24% max
  drawdown** (2009–2024) — modest return, but the drawdown profile run 58 needed, and a
  selection rule that is precisely a "push likelihood" score. (One replication critique found
  weak short-horizon predictive power; the claim that survives is the *portfolio* profile, not
  per-signal alpha.)
- **Da–Gurun–Warachka, "Frog in the Pan" (RFS 2014)**: momentum among stocks whose gains arrived
  *continuously* runs **+5.94%**, versus **−2.07%** for the same cumulative gain arriving in
  discrete jumps. Smoothness is the quality signal. This is the scientific backing for the
  `trend_vol` arm Zak pre-registered today, and it says our junk book (gappy movers that made
  one violent high) is exactly the population the literature predicts loses.
- Our own C1 lesson agrees from the other side: every §3.2 gate measured anti-predictive against
  the population that produces 70% moves. Gates must be *measured on the target population*
  before adoption — which we can now do mechanically (see §4, the push study).

### The ride → exits on trend death, not on noise

- Run 58's one clean catch (ASTS, 74.7% of the move kept) proves the machinery can ride. What
  clips the rest is the *initial* stop, not the trail. Clenow's answer: no stop at entry-noise
  distance; the position leaves when the trend statistically dies (ranking decay / MA break).
- The practitioners split the position instead of choosing: **Qullamaggie** (verified
  eight-figure track) sells 1/3–1/2 into strength on days 3–5, moves the stop to breakeven, and
  trails the remainder on the **10- or 20-day moving average**, exiting on a close below.
  Notably his *entry precondition* is a name that already ran 30–100% and then consolidated
  orderly for 2–8 weeks — he demands a demonstrated push before he'll touch it. Our audit's
  push ledger is literally a catalogue of his raw material. **Minervini**: bank at least half at
  2–3× the initial risk, never round-trip a multiple-R gain.
- Both practitioner schemes and Clenow agree on the load-bearing point: **the wide trail is
  earned by banked profit or proven trend, never granted at entry** — A2 granted it at entry
  and paid for it twice (the junk rode nothing; the leaders never survived to the trail).

### The tail → volatility-managed exposure

- **Barroso & Santa-Clara, "Momentum Has Its Moments" (JFE 2015)**: momentum's crash risk is
  forecastable *by its own recent realized volatility*. Scaling exposure to target constant
  volatility (~12%, scaled by inverse 6-month realized vol) took the strategy's Sharpe from
  **0.53 to 0.97**, cut excess kurtosis from **18.24 to 2.68**, pulled left skew from **−2.47 to
  −0.42**, and "virtually eliminated" the crashes. **Daniel & Moskowitz (JFE 2016)** improved it
  further with dynamic scaling — roughly doubling alpha and Sharpe.
- Run 58's daily-return kurtosis (~14 on our own tape) and −62% bootstrap tail is this exact
  disease, unmanaged. The repo already parked this idea as **WO-17, the vol governor**,
  "revisit at the bear-gate stage." The evidence says it is not an accessory to revisit later —
  for this strategy family it is load-bearing. Its revival is Zak's ruling to make.
- We already own the other half of tail defense: the M1 regime gate (index above its long MA)
  is Clenow's rule verbatim, and it is already in `signals.market_gate`.

## 4 · The proposed play, in order of cost

1. **E4 now (cheap, sanctioned, next in the execution order).** VOO core + SPMO tilt
   {100/0, 90/10, 80/20}, §2.1 banding, §2.5 bars. SPMO's bars are in the store (2,351 bars,
   2015-10 → yesterday). Needs a small blend simulator (a dispatch script; the momentum engine
   is not involved) and sets the buy-side bar every active arm must beat. If nothing beats it,
   the work order already names the honest terminus: hold the blend.
2. **The push study (our proprietary edge — measure before adopting).** The audit left us a
   labeled dataset no paper has: **2,178 completed pushes and every failed breakout on our own
   census, 2017–2026.** Score every breakout day on the candidate gates — prior-run size +
   consolidation orderliness (Qullamaggie's precondition), 90-day slope × R² (Clenow),
   information-discreteness / smoothness (FIP), ADDV band, market regime — and measure each
   gate's lift on P(push completes) and P(ride survives), exactly the way C1's census screen
   was built ("the same census read forwards"). The gates that survive become A3's
   pre-registered spec; the ones that don't, die before costing a trial.
3. **A3 — "hold the leaders," the pre-registered family (one WO, Zak-approved, doctrine
   upstream).** The shape the evidence converges on: selection by measured trend quality
   (slope × R² and/or FIP-smoothness per the push study), exits on trend death (ranking decay /
   own long MA) instead of an entry-noise stop — optionally the practitioner hybrid
   (partial bank into strength, then MA-trail) as one arm — vol-targeted exposure per
   Barroso–Santa-Clara (WO-17 revived), on the proven park chassis. Center + small
   pre-registered ladder, §2.5 bars, capture audit on every run, E-final bear gate before any
   production candidacy.

The three fixes map one-to-one onto the three failure buckets, which is the test of a diagnosis
rather than a shopping list.

## Biases and honesty footer

Sourced numbers above are the papers' and replicators' claims, not our runs; replication figures
for Clenow-style systems vary widely with universe and era, and at least one replication finds
weak per-signal predictive power. Championship figures are contest-audited single years, not
compounded track records. SPMO's ten-year record includes no full bear market longer than 2022;
our own E-final gate exists for exactly that reason. Nothing here has cleared §2.5 on our tape;
per the scout rule, all of it is existence proof, none of it is a finding.
