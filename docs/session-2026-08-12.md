## 2. Proven correct (previously assumed)

- **ADDV is free of future-split inflation.** It uses `adj_close × volume`, where the split factors
  cancel. **Run 18 did not** — it used `raw_close × volume`, inflating a name's past liquidity by
  its own future split factor. CMG's pre-split ADDV read $79bn; 660 names affected; 170,220
  name-days entered L0 on that alone. **The old runs held the look-ahead; the new ones removed it.**
- **The $5 price floor reads the raw print**, which is the one clause that wants an actual level
  rather than a restated one.
- **The whole bar is scaled by one shared adjustment factor**, so intraday relationships and
  cross-time ratios both survive. `raw_close` is read in exactly two places.
- **EPS is point-in-time on `reportDate`**, not period end — `eps_as_of()` is a prefix cut on filing
  date. This is the trap `.claude/rules/trading-code.md` names, and the engine handles it.

### Now asserted on every run, so it cannot silently regress

1. **The books balance** — closing cash equals `start_nav + Σ realised P&L`, exactly. Park P&L is
   carried in explicitly so the capital layer cannot disable the check.
2. **The sleeve never borrows** — negative cash halts. Sizing is a fraction of NAV while the money
   available is cash, and nothing in the fill path enforced the difference.
3. **The tape is the adjusted series** — halts if the decision series reads as the raw print.
4. **Duplicates compared element-wise**, and broken securities quarantined under a 10% ceiling.

**Known boundary:** these prove money is *conserved*, not that positions are *priced* correctly.
Defect #6 passed every accounting check — cash and holdings were both right, only the mark was
wrong, and a mark is invisible to a cash identity. **A drawdown is a valuation statistic.**

## 5. Findings

**F1 — Only the capital layer beat the benchmark.** Six of seven arms lose badly to buying VOO. The
best *strategy* arm compounds at 5.15% against the index's 15.44%. The one arm that wins changed
where idle cash sits, not which names were bought.

**F2 — A1's headline is one trade.** 71.4% of A1's entire profit is **MU, +538% over 239 days**. Top
three = 96.1%; top five = 106.4%, meaning the other 91 trades are net negative. §7i of the
2026-08-10 findings said the same from the other side: *"the result is one position, and it is not
closed."*

**F3 — The gap between per-trade edge and account return is entirely sizing.** Four of five arms
have positive expectancy; only two beat the baseline. And it runs the wrong way — the arms that stay
small survive, the one that deploys blows up.

**F4 — M1 is the sharpest evidence for that.** It is the only arm taking real size (41% exposure
against 8–17%), it has **positive expectancy of +2.21%**, and it still **loses 8.07% with a −39.89%
drawdown.** A positive edge and a negative account is over-betting a mediocre strategy. This is the
second recording of the pattern — §7f already had *"a real edge by every per-trade measure, and a
−53.5% drawdown."*

**F5 — C1 vs C3 isolates the exit, not the screen.** Near-identical win rates (49.7% vs 49.2%),
expectancy down two-thirds (+9.80% → +3.66%), return +17.1% → +2.6%. Same entries, same hit rate,
very different payoff. The cheapest and narrowest lead available.

**F6 — C1 wins often and small, then gives it back.** Read absolutes, not ratios: A1's top ten make
$144k while the rest lose $31k; **C1's top ten make $103k while the rest lose $69k.** Its 49.7% win
rate is real and is not what drives the result.

**F7 — The capital rule is proven; the sleeve is not.** A1V returns +295.25% against a
**90/10 VOO/cash counterfactual's +222.10%** — the sleeve adds 73 points over nine years, ~2.7% a
year, with a shallower drawdown than the index. But momentum itself did not change (97 trades vs 96,
identical entries by test), so the gain is the 74% that stopped sitting in cash. And the 73-point
contribution inherits F2's single-name dependency.
