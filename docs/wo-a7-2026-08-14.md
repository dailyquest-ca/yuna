## 1 · Why the B-arm does not run, and this does

WO-A6 §5's B-arm tranches the **bi-monthly** clock. Measured against ruling 4 it should not run,
and the arithmetic is not close: the bi-monthly phase mean is **20.58%** — below SPMO — and
tranching collects a mean rather than a maximum. Its ceiling is roughly the ETF. It stays built,
tested and unrun.

The **weekly** clock is where a 30% number already exists. All five phases are stored:

**Three of the five carry a Sharpe above SPMO's 0.987.** The mean is pulled under it by one phase,
`s5_p3`, which is 14.1 points below the best. This document has previously called the weekly clock
"leverage, not edge" on the strength of that 0.970 mean. That reading is too flat: three phases are
edge and one is not, and the arm's real problem is that **you get exactly one of them, and which
one is decided by the day of the week you happened to start on.**

That is the same date-luck the whole programme has been chasing, and tranching is the instrument
for it — the one built for WO-A6 §5 and pointed at the wrong clock.

## 2 · Construction

`every_sessions=1, tranches=5`. Every session is a rebalance date; `turn = ord % 5`, so tranche *k*
rebalances on sessions congruent to *k* mod 5. **Five weekly books on five different weekly phases,
held simultaneously.** Each owns `n/5` slots and funds its own fifth of NAV, so per-name weight
matches the single-tranche book of the same N.

N moves **8 → 10** so the five tranches divide evenly at two names each. That is why the controls
exist: `w10_p0…p4` are the five weekly phases *at N=10*, so the tranched cell is priced against its
own phase mean. Comparing a ten-name tranched book against the stored eight-name phases would move
two axes and prove nothing.

Six cells: `w10_p0, w10_p1, w10_p2, w10_p3, w10_p4, w10_t5`.

## 3 · Predictions, committed before the results

The thesis is that **tranching converts a lottery into its own expected value.** If it is right:

1. **`w10_t5` CAGR lands within ±3 points of the mean of the five `w10_p*` controls.** This is the
   central claim and the one most likely to fail. Materially *below* means tranching leaks —
   cross-tranche name rotation, a name sold by one tranche and re-bought by another, is real and
   costs spread. Materially *above* is not a win to celebrate: it would mean a rebalancing bonus I
   did not predict, and the first response is to look for a defect, not to bank it.
2. **`w10_t5` Sharpe ≥ the control-mean Sharpe.** This is the actual reason to do it. The five
   phases hold overlapping books offset by days, so they are highly but not perfectly correlated;
   a blend of imperfectly correlated series has lower volatility than the average of its parts. If
   the blended Sharpe does *not* beat the component average, the phases are effectively the same
   series and tranching buys nothing but complexity.
3. **Turnover stays in the controls' range**, ~250–310 names/yr. A tranched book trades a fifth as
   many names per date on five times as many dates. If it comes in near 5× the controls, names are
   rotating between tranches and the construction is churning rather than diversifying.

**What I do not predict:** that this beats A6 risk-adjusted. The weekly clock takes a −51%
bootstrap drawdown against A6's −36.6%. Ruling 1 accepts drawdown; it does not make it free, and
the comparison belongs in the decision line rather than being assumed away here.

## 4 · Pass bars and kills

- **Meets ruling 4** if `w10_t5` CAGR ≥ **30%** — Zak's stated target, phase-independent by
  construction.
- **Beats the ETF risk-adjusted** if Sharpe > **0.989** (SPMO on the identical window). Three of
  the five phases already do; the question is whether the blend does.
- **Kill — tranching leaks:** `w10_t5` more than 3 points below the control mean. Then the weekly
  arm is a lottery you cannot convert, and the honest options are to play one phase and accept the
  draw, or to leave it.
- **Kill — churn:** turnover > 400 names/yr. Ruling 2 relaxed the flag; it did not remove it.
- **Not a bar:** the deflated Sharpe. Ruling 3 retired it. It is still computed, still recorded,
  and these six cells will lower it for every arm in the ledger — including A6's. That is the
  correct behaviour of the statistic and the accepted cost of ruling 4.

## 5 · Decision line

`w10_t5` clears §4 and beats A6 on both return and Sharpe → it replaces A6 as the live candidate,
and the plan amendment names it. Clears the 30% but not the Sharpe → a two-arm decision for Zak,
explicitly framed as more compounding for more drawdown, which is the trade he has said three times
he wants. Fails §4 → A6 stands as the candidate at family-mean expectations, and the weekly clock
is closed out as a lottery rather than an arm.
